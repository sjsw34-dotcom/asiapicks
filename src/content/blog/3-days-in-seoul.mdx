Seoul doesn't ease you in gently — it grabs you by the collar the moment you step off the airport express and says *welcome, keep up*. Ancient palaces sit in the shadow of glass skyscrapers. Buddhist monks share streets with K-pop fans in full idol merch. The food is extraordinary, the transport is borderline miraculous, and three days is just enough time to fall completely in love with it. Here's how to spend every one of those hours well.

## Day 1: Palaces, Hanok Villages & the Soul of Old Seoul

### Morning — Gyeongbokgung Palace & Bukchon

Start early. **Gyeongbokgung Palace** opens at 9am and the crowds don't hit full force until after 10:30am. Entry is a steal at around **$3 USD** (3,000 KRW), and if you rent a *hanbok* (traditional Korean dress) from one of the rental shops lining the north gate, you get in **free**. Hanbok rentals run about **$8–12 USD** for two hours — absolutely worth it for the photos alone.

From the palace, walk northeast into **Bukchon Hanok Village**. This is one of Seoul's most photographed neighborhoods, a hillside labyrinth of restored 600-year-old tile-roofed homes. It's free to wander and genuinely stunning, but be respectful — people actually live here, so keep noise down and don't peer into courtyards.

### Afternoon — Insadong & Cheonggyecheon Stream

Head south to **Insadong**, Seoul's artsy cultural corridor. The main street is lined with tea houses, galleries, and craft shops. Duck into **Ssamziegil** (44 Insadong-gil), a quirky open-air mall built around a spiral courtyard — grab a *hotteok* (sweet pancake, about **$1.50 USD**) from a street vendor here.

Wind down the afternoon with a walk along **Cheonggyecheon Stream**, a beautifully restored urban waterway that cuts through central Seoul. It's about 11km long but even a 30-minute stroll from the Gwanghwamun end is refreshing and surprisingly peaceful given that you're in the middle of a megacity.

### Evening — Gwangjang Market

For dinner, there's nowhere better on night one than **Gwangjang Market** (88 Changgyeonggung-ro, Jongno-gu). This covered traditional market has been running since 1905 and the food alley inside is electric. Try *bindaetteok* (mung bean pancakes, ~**$4 USD**), fresh *mayak gimbap* (~**$2 USD**), and if you're adventurous, *yukhoe* (raw beef tartare). Wash it down with *makgeolli* (rice wine) for a few dollars a bowl. This is the real Seoul.

## Day 2: Modern Seoul — Han River, Hongdae & Gangnam

### Morning — Han River Park

Locals come to **Yeouido Han River Park** on weekends like it's a religion. Rent a bicycle (~**$5 USD/hour**) from the rental station near Yeouido Station (Line 5 or 9) and cruise along the river. Pick up cup noodles from a convenience store — yes, the GS25 or CU on the riverside — and eat them on the grass like a true Seoulite. It costs almost nothing and feels like everything.

### Afternoon — Hongdae & Sinchon

**Hongdae** is Seoul's university arts district, and even by day it buzzes. Browse the indie clothing shops, vintage stores, and record shops around **Hongik University Station** (Line 2). This is also the best neighborhood to book street food tours through **Klook** — their Hongdae street food and culture experiences run around **$30–45 USD** per person and cover spots most travelers walk right past.

From Hongdae, take Line 2 south to **Gangnam** for a late afternoon contrast. Walk the famous **Garosu-gil** tree-lined street in Sinchon district, pop into flagship beauty stores like Innisfree and Amorepacific, and soak in the polished, upscale energy that defines Seoul's south side.

### Evening — Rooftop Bar in Itaewon

**Itaewon** is Seoul's most internationally flavored neighborhood and its nightlife hub. For sunset drinks, **Cakeshop Rooftop** or the terraces around **Haebangchon (HBC)** offer brilliant views of Namsan Tower. Dinner here is an adventure — the neighborhood has everything from Lebanese to Ethiopian to modern Korean fusion. Budget around **$15–25 USD** for a solid sit-down meal.

## Day 3: Hidden Gems — Bukhansan, Mangwon & Sunset at N Seoul Tower

### Morning — Bukhansan National Park

On your final morning, wake up early and get out of the city — or rather, into the mountain that sits *inside* the city. **Bukhansan National Park** is a 40-minute subway ride from central Seoul (take Line 3 to Gupabal Station) and offers serious hiking with granite peaks and Buddhist temples tucked between the ridges. The **Baegundae Peak** trail takes about 3–4 hours round trip. Entry is free. Bring water and good shoes — it's genuinely steep in places.

### Afternoon — Mangwon Market & Yeonnam-dong

Back in the city, cool off in the charming neighborhood of **Yeonnam-dong**, just east of Hongdae. This is where Seoul's creative class actually hangs out — independent cafés, natural wine bars, and zero tourist traps. Lunch at **Mangwon Market** (nearby, accessible from Mangwon Station on Line 6) gives you the local market experience without Gwangjang's tourist crowds. Street snacks here run **$1–4 USD** each.

### Evening — N Seoul Tower

End your Seoul trip exactly where the city wants you to end it: on top of **Namsan Mountain** at **N Seoul Tower**. Take the cable car up (~**$12 USD** return) or hike the 20-minute trail through Namsan Park if you still have energy. The observation deck entry is around **$12 USD** separately, though buying a combo ticket on **Klook** beforehand saves you a few dollars and skips the queue.

Watch the city light up below you — 10 million people, thousands of years of history, and three days that somehow felt like both a sprint and a dream.

For the best rates, checking **Agoda** is a solid move — they consistently have strong deals on Korean hotels, particularly in Myeongdong and Insadong where location really matters for a short trip.

## Practical Tips for First-Time Seoul Visitors

- **Get a T-money card** at the airport or any convenience store (~$2.50 USD). Load it with credit and use it for all subway and bus travel. Single trips cost around **$1.20–1.50 USD**.
- **AREX Airport Express** connects Incheon Airport to central Seoul in 43 minutes for about **$9 USD**. Don't bother with a taxi for this leg.
- **Pocket WiFi or SIM card**: Pick one up at the airport. Pocket WiFi rentals run around **$5–7 USD/day**; a local SIM is often cheaper for longer stays.
- **Kakao Maps** works better than Google Maps for navigating Seoul's transit system. Download it before you go.
- **Tap water is safe** to drink in Seoul — a rarity in Asia that saves you money and plastic.
- **Tipping is not expected** and can occasionally cause mild confusion. Save your change for more street food.
- Most attractions are **closed on Mondays** — plan accordingly, especially for Gyeongbokgung.

## Final Thoughts

Three days in Seoul will leave you deeply satisfied and slightly overwhelmed — in the best possible way. The city rewards curiosity: the more you wander off the main drag, the better it gets. You'll eat spectacularly for very little money, move around with ease on one of the world's great transit systems, and come away with the distinct feeling that Seoul is a city that takes being a city *very* seriously. It's hard not to respect that. And even harder not to start planning your return trip on the flight home.
