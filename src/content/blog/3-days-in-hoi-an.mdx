If there's one town in Southeast Asia that genuinely lives up to the hype, it's Hoi An. The lantern-lit alleyways, the Thu Bon River at golden hour, the smell of *cao lầu* wafting out of century-old shophouses — this place has a way of making you slow down and actually *feel* somewhere. Three days here isn't a lot, but with the right plan, it's enough to fall completely in love. Here's exactly how to spend them.

## Getting to Hoi An

Hoi An doesn't have its own airport, so most travelers fly into **Da Nang International Airport (DAD)**, which is about 30 km away. From there:

- **Taxi/Grab:** ~$8–12 USD, around 45 minutes. Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber) is reliable and the easiest option.
- **Private transfer:** ~$15–20 USD, worth booking in advance if you're arriving late. You can arrange these through your hotel or on **Klook** for a fixed, stress-free price.
- **Mini-bus shuttle:** ~$3–5 USD, takes longer due to stops but works fine if you're on a budget.

Once you're in Hoi An, the Ancient Town is entirely walkable. Rent a bicycle (~$1–2/day from any guesthouse) for exploring further out, or use Grab for rainy days.

## Day 1: Lose Yourself in the Ancient Town

### Morning — Ancient Town on Your Own Terms

Start early. The Ancient Town is at its most magical before 9am, when the tour groups haven't arrived yet and the light is soft and golden. Grab a **bánh mì** from **Bánh Mì Phượng** (2B Phan Châu Trinh — widely considered the best in Vietnam, ~$1.50 USD) and eat it while wandering.

Your first stops should be:

- **Japanese Covered Bridge (Lai Viễn Kiều):** The symbol of Hoi An. Get there early for photos without a crowd.
- **Tan Ky Ancient House** (101 Nguyen Thai Hoc): A beautifully preserved 200-year-old merchant home, entry included in the Ancient Town ticket ($5 USD, covers 5 heritage sites).
- **Phúc Kiến Assembly Hall:** Stunning Chinese temple with elaborate architecture and incense smoke curling through the air.

### Afternoon — Get Something Made

Hoi An is famous for custom tailoring, and honestly, it's worth the experience. A decent tailored dress runs **$30–60 USD**, a suit **$80–150 USD**, depending on fabric. Give yourself at least 2 days for fittings.

Reputable shops include **Yaly Couture** (47 Nguyen Thi Minh Khai) and **A Dong Silk** (40 Le Loi). Bring inspiration photos and be specific about what you want.

After your fitting appointment, wander down to the **Thu Bon Riverfront** as the afternoon light turns everything amber. This is prime café-hopping territory — pull up a chair at **The Cargo Club** (107-109 Nguyen Thai Hoc) for river views and a cold Vietnamese iced coffee (~$2).

### Evening — Lantern Night

If your visit falls on the 14th or 15th of the lunar month, the **Hoi An Lantern Festival** turns the Ancient Town car-free and candlelit. Even on regular evenings, the lanterns are beautiful. Buy a floating lantern (~$1) from a riverside vendor, make a wish, and send it down the river.

Dinner recommendation: **Mì Quảng Bà Mua** (near the market area, Tran Phu St) for a local bowl of *mì quảng* (turmeric noodles with pork and herbs) for about **$2 USD**. Zero pretense, maximum flavor.

## Day 2: Cooking Classes, Beach & Hidden Villages

### Morning — Learn to Cook Vietnamese Food

This is the one activity you shouldn't skip. A Hoi An cooking class is genuinely one of the best half-days you can spend in Vietnam. Most classes include a market tour where you learn to identify local ingredients, followed by hands-on cooking of 4–5 dishes.

**Morning Glory Cooking School** (run by celebrity chef Trinh Diem Vy, 106 Nguyen Thai Hoc) is the gold standard — classes from **$38 USD**. **Red Bridge Cooking School** offers a boat ride to its riverside kitchen for a more scenic experience (~$35–45 USD). Both are bookable through **Klook**, which often has small discounts and instant confirmation.

### Afternoon — An Bang Beach

After lunch (which you cooked yourself — congrats), head 8 km east to **An Bang Beach**. It's quieter and more local-feeling than Da Nang's beaches, with a string of laid-back beach bars. **Soul Kitchen** and **Coconut Beach Bar** rent sunbeds for free if you buy a drink or two. A fresh coconut will run you about **$1.50**, a cold beer around **$2**.

Grab a Grab back to town or rent a bicycle for the scenic ride through rice paddies.

### Evening — Pick Up Your Tailored Pieces

Head back for your tailoring fitting — most shops schedule this 24–36 hours after the first appointment. Then reward yourself with dinner at **Streets Restaurant Café** (17 Le Loi), a social enterprise that trains disadvantaged youth in hospitality. The food is excellent and you'll feel good about spending $10–15 USD there.

## Day 3: Day Trip to My Son Sanctuary or the Villages

### Option A — My Son Sanctuary (History Lovers)

About 40 km from Hoi An, **My Son Sanctuary** is a cluster of ancient Cham Hindu temples dating back to the 4th century. It's often called Vietnam's Angkor Wat (a bit of a stretch, but the atmosphere is genuinely mystical). Entry is **$15 USD**.

Go on a half-day guided tour — the context makes it dramatically more interesting. Klook has well-reviewed tours departing from Hoi An from around **$18–25 USD** including transport.

### Option B — Tra Que Vegetable Village (Food & Nature Lovers)

Just 3 km from the Ancient Town, **Tra Que Village** is where Hoi An's legendary herbs are grown. You can join a **farm-to-table cooking experience** ($20–30 USD) where you harvest vegetables, cook them, then eat lunch surrounded by green fields. Deeply peaceful, and a world away from the tourist bustle.

### Last Afternoon — Café Crawl & Souvenir Shopping

Spend your final afternoon slowly. **Rosie's Café** (46 Nguyen Phuc Tan) does exceptional Vietnamese pour-over coffee. The **Hoi An Market** (Tran Phu) is chaotic and wonderful for last-minute ceramics, silk pouches, and lanterns.

## Practical Tips for Hoi An

- **Ancient Town ticket:** $5 USD covers 5 sites. Buy at any of the ticket booths near the main entrances. Some sites require separate entry.
- **Best time to visit:** February–April (dry, warm, not peak-humid). Avoid October–November — serious flooding risk in the Ancient Town.
- **Cash is king:** Many small restaurants and market stalls don't accept cards. Stock up at ATMs on Tran Hung Dao St. Expect to pay ~$3–4 USD in ATM fees.
- **Bargaining:** Expected at markets, not at restaurants. Start at about 50–60% of the asking price.
- **Scams to know:** Motorbike taxi drivers may quote prices in VND but mean USD. Confirm currency before you get on.
- **Electricity:** Vietnam uses 220V. European two-pin plugs work fine; US travelers need a universal adapter.
- **Dress code:** Cover shoulders and knees for temple visits. Keep a light scarf in your bag.

## Final Thoughts

Three days in Hoi An goes fast, but if you stay present — skip a few tourist boxes in favor of a riverside coffee, a conversation with a tailor, an early morning walk before the crowds — you'll leave with more than just photos. You'll leave with a feeling. And that's what this town specializes in.
