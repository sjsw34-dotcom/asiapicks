Taipei doesn't ease you in gently — it grabs you by the collar the moment you step off the MRT and pulls you into a city that somehow makes ancient temples, futuristic skyscrapers, and some of the world's best street food feel like the most natural combination imaginable. Three days here won't cover everything (nothing will), but with the right plan you'll leave feeling like you genuinely *got* the city — not just ticked boxes at it.

## Day 1: Historic Heart — Temples, Tea, and the Best Night Market on Earth

### Morning: Longshan Temple & Wanhua District

Start early (before 9 AM if you can) at **Longshan Temple** in the Wanhua district — Taipei's oldest and most atmospheric place of worship. Entrance is free, incense smoke hangs thick in the air, and local devotees genuinely come here to pray, not to perform for tourists. Spend 30–45 minutes soaking it in, then wander the surrounding lanes of **Bopiliao Historic Block**, a preserved Qing Dynasty street that feels a world away from the glass towers a few kilometers east.

From there, grab breakfast at one of the nearby street stalls — a bowl of *doujiang* (warm soy milk) and a *shaobing* (sesame flatbread) will set you back around **$1.50–$2 USD** and fuel you properly for the morning.

### Afternoon: Ximending & Da'an Forest Park

Take the MRT to **Ximending** — Taipei's answer to Tokyo's Harajuku, buzzing with street performers, bubble tea shops, and independent fashion boutiques. It's lively, colorful, and great for people-watching. Grab a cup of **tiger sugar boba** (~$2.50) from one of the originals here.

By mid-afternoon, head east to **Da'an Forest Park** for a breather. Locals jog, do tai chi, and nap under the trees here — it's an honest slice of daily Taipei life.

### Evening: Shilin Night Market

No first night in Taipei is complete without **Shilin Night Market** (MRT: Jiantan station). It's touristy, yes — but it's touristy because it's genuinely excellent. Must-eats:

- **Oyster omelette** (*o-a-tsian*) — ~$3
- **Large fried chicken cutlet** — ~$2.50
- **Stinky tofu** — ~$2 (trust us, just try it)
- **Mango shaved ice** — ~$3–4

Budget around **$10–15 USD** for a proper Shilin feast. Come hungry.

## Day 2: Iconic Taipei — Taipei 101, Art, and Mountain Views

### Morning: Taipei 101 Observatory

Get to **Taipei 101** when it opens at 9 AM to beat the crowds. The observatory ticket costs **$18 USD** and the views across the Taipei basin — with mountains ringing the city on every side — are genuinely spectacular. Book tickets in advance on **Klook** to skip the queue and often save a few dollars.

### Afternoon: Yongkang Street & the National Palace Museum

After Taipei 101, walk 10 minutes north to **Yongkang Street** — the city's most charming café and restaurant strip. Have lunch at **Din Tai Fung's original flagship** (No. 194, Xinyi Road, Section 2) — yes, there will be a queue, but the *xiaolongbao* (soup dumplings) here are transcendent. Budget **$15–20 USD** per person for a proper meal.

In the afternoon, Uber or taxi (~$10) to the **National Palace Museum** in Shilin. It houses one of the largest collections of Chinese imperial artifacts in the world — the carved jade cabbage alone is worth the trip. Entry is **$4.50 USD**. Allow 2–3 hours minimum.

### Evening: Raohe Street Night Market

A more local alternative to Shilin, **Raohe Street Night Market** (MRT: Songshan station) is shorter, less chaotic, and has arguably better food. Don't miss the **black pepper buns** from the famous stall right at the temple entrance — they bake them in a clay oven and hand them out piping hot for about **$2 each**.

## Day 3: Escape the City — Jiufen, Maokong & Farewell Bites

### Morning: Day Trip to Jiufen

Take a 40–50 minute bus from Taipei Main Station (Bus 1062, ~$2.50 each way) up into the mountains to **Jiufen** — the gold-rush hillside village that inspired the aesthetic of *Spirited Away*. Wander the famous **Old Street**, duck into tea houses perched over the valley, and buy *taro balls* from a street stall for around **$3**. Go early; by noon it fills up fast.

### Afternoon: Maokong Gondola & Tea Tasting

Back in Taipei by mid-afternoon, take the MRT to Zhantan and then the **Maokong Gondola** (round trip ~$5) up into the tea-growing hills above the city. Several traditional teahouses here serve **high mountain oolong** with light snacks — budget **$8–12 per person** for a relaxed hour of tea culture with panoramic city views below.

### Evening: Final Feast in Zhongshan

End your Taipei trip in style in the **Zhongshan** neighborhood — sophisticated, a little artsy, and packed with excellent restaurants and rooftop bars. Splurge on a farewell dinner at one of the Japanese-Taiwanese fusion spots along **Chifeng Street**, or keep it street-level with a final round of beef noodle soup (~$5–7) at a local noodle house. Either way, you won't leave hungry.

## Where to Stay in Taipei

Taipei's MRT system is so good that location matters less than in most Asian cities — but **Da'an**, **Zhongshan**, and **Xinyi** districts are the sweet spots for first-timers. A solid mid-range hotel runs **$60–120/night**. Check **Agoda** for competitive rates and last-minute deals, particularly for boutique hotels that often aren't on other platforms.

| Area | Best For | Price Range |

## Practical Tips for Taipei

- **Get an EasyCard** at any MRT station on arrival (~$5 deposit) — it covers the metro, buses, and YouBike bike rentals across the city.
- **Cash is still king** at night markets. ATMs are everywhere and dispense New Taiwan Dollars (TWD); $1 USD ≈ 32 TWD.
- **Weather matters:** Taipei is hot and humid June–September; bring a compact umbrella as afternoon showers are common. October–December is the sweet spot for weather.
- **Google Maps works perfectly** for transit directions — use it over paper maps.
- **Uber is reliable and affordable** for cross-city trips where the MRT isn't direct; typical ride is $4–8 USD.
- **7-Eleven and FamilyMart** are genuinely good here — hot food, ATMs, SIM cards, and cold beers at midnight. They'll be your best friends.
- Book popular activities like Taipei 101 skip-the-line tickets and guided food tours through **Klook** for convenience and bundled savings.

## Getting Around Taipei

The **Taipei Metro (MRT)** is fast, clean, air-conditioned, and bilingual — it should be your default for most journeys. A single trip rarely exceeds **$1.20 USD**. The airport MRT connects **Taoyuan Airport (TPE)** directly to Taipei Main Station in about 35 minutes (~$5 USD), making arrival and departure completely painless.

Taipei rewards slow walkers and spontaneous turns down side streets. The best discoveries here — a grandmother selling *tanguyuan* from a cart, a courtyard temple hidden behind a scooter repair shop — aren't on any map. Leave room in the itinerary for getting happily lost.
