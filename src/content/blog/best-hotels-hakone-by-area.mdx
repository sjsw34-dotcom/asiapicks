Hakone is one of those rare places that genuinely lives up to the hype. Nestled in the Fuji-Hakone-Izu National Park about 90 minutes from Tokyo, this mountain resort town delivers steaming hot springs, sweeping views of Mount Fuji, fresh mountain air, and some of the most memorable accommodation experiences in all of Japan. Whether you're dreaming of soaking in a private *rotenburo* (outdoor bath) while mist rolls through cedar forests, or simply want a comfortable base to explore the area's volcanic landscapes and art museums, **where you stay in Hakone makes all the difference**. This guide breaks it all down by area so you can find your perfect match.

## Understanding Hakone: Why Area Matters

Hakone isn't a single town — it's a sprawling collection of villages, valleys, and lakeshores spread across a volcanic caldera. The Hakone Tozan Railway, the ropeway, lake ferries, and buses all connect the dots, but **your home base dramatically shapes your experience**. Staying near Hakone-Yumoto means easy train access from Tokyo but a more touristy feel. Staying by Lake Ashi puts you in postcard territory with Fuji views. Staying in Gora or Miyanoshita lands you somewhere quieter, with a classic mountain resort vibe.

If you're booking hotels, **Agoda** consistently has strong rates for Hakone properties — especially the mid-range ryokans that don't always appear on Western booking platforms. It's worth checking there first.

## Hakone-Yumoto: Best for Easy Access & Budget Stays

Hakone-Yumoto is the gateway to Hakone — the first stop on the Hakone Tozan Railway from Odawara, and where most day-trippers first arrive. It has the most concentrated strip of onsen hotels and ryokans, making it ideal for **first-time visitors or those on a tighter budget**.

### Top Picks in Hakone-Yumoto

- **Yumoto Fujiya Hotel** — A reliable mid-range hotel with indoor and outdoor onsen baths, meals included in most plans. Rates from around **$120–$180/night** for two including dinner and breakfast. Address: 256 Yumoto, Hakone-machi.
- **Kowakien Tenyu** — More of a splurge, but the open-air baths and kaiseki dinners are exceptional. Expect to pay **$300–$450/night** per person for full board. A proper treat if it's your first ryokan experience.
- **Hakone Tent** — A stylish guesthouse popular with younger travelers and solo visitors. Simple Japanese rooms, communal onsen, and a lively common area. From **$80–$120/night**. Conveniently located a short walk from Hakone-Yumoto Station.

**Getting there:** Direct Romancecar express train from Shinjuku to Hakone-Yumoto takes about 85 minutes (~$15 USD one-way). Regular trains via Odawara also work and are cheaper.

## Miyanoshita & Gora: Best for Classic Ryokan Atmosphere

Ride the charming rack railway a few stops up the mountain and you enter a different era entirely. **Miyanoshita and Gora** are where Hakone's old-money resort culture lives — think manicured gardens, moss-covered stone paths, and ryokans that have been welcoming guests for over a century.

### Top Picks in Miyanoshita & Gora

- **Fujiya Hotel (Miyanoshita)** — One of Japan's most historic Western-style hotels, opened in 1878. It's been fully renovated and blends colonial charm with modern comfort. Doubles from **$200–$350/night**. The dining room alone is worth the stay. Address: 359 Miyanoshita, Hakone.
- **Hakone Ginyu (Gora)** — A luxury ryokan with in-room private onsen baths and floor-to-ceiling forest views. This is a splurge — rates start at around **$500–$700/night per person** including two meals — but it's consistently ranked among Japan's finest. Address: 1300-681 Ninotaira, Hakone.
- **Gora Kadan** — Another top-tier ryokan set in a former imperial villa. Impeccable kaiseki cuisine, private baths, and immaculate service. From **$600+/night per person** with meals. Not cheap, but genuinely unforgettable.

**Getting there:** Hakone Tozan Railway from Hakone-Yumoto to Gora takes about 40 minutes. Most ryokans offer free shuttle pickups from the station — always confirm in advance.

## Lake Ashi (Hakone-en & Moto-Hakone): Best for Fuji Views

If you've seen that iconic image of Mount Fuji reflected in a calm lake with a vermillion torii gate in the foreground — that's Lake Ashi. Staying here means waking up (on clear mornings) to one of the world's great views. It's also the most *romantic* area of Hakone, and tends to attract couples and photographers.

### Top Picks Around Lake Ashi

- **Hakone Prince Hotel** — A sprawling lakeside resort with multiple wings, multiple onsen, and direct lake views. Great for families too. Doubles from **$180–$280/night**. Address: 144 Moto-Hakone, Hakone.
- **Hyatt Regency Hakone Resort & Spa** — The most polished international-brand option in the area, with stunning lake-facing rooms and a world-class spa. Rates from **$350–$550/night**. Address: 1320 Gora, Hakone (shuttle to lake area available).
- **Moto-Hakone Guesthouse** — For budget travelers who still want the lakeside location, this simple guesthouse delivers clean rooms and a friendly atmosphere from around **$70–$100/night**.

**Getting there:** Take the Hakone Tozan Bus from Hakone-Yumoto or Odawara to Moto-Hakone or Hakone-en stops. The lake ferry (*Hakone Sightseeing Cruise*) is a gorgeous way to arrive — book it through **Klook** for easy ticketing and occasional discounts.

## Sengokuhara: Best for a Quiet, Local Feel

Sengokuhara sits on Hakone's northern plateau, away from the main tourist trail. It's known for its famous *susuki* pampas grass fields (spectacular in autumn), the Pola Museum of Art, and a growing cluster of boutique stays. If you want to escape the crowds while still being well-connected, **this is your area**.

### Top Picks in Sengokuhara

- **Hatsuhana Sengokuhara** — A well-regarded mid-range ryokan with private and communal onsen and hearty Japanese meals. From **$200–$350/night per person** with meals.
- **Sengokuhara Onsen Ryokan Kikka** — Smaller and more intimate, this is a great pick for couples. Traditional tatami rooms, in-room yukata, and attentive service. From **$180–$260/night**.

**Getting there:** Buses run from Hakone-Yumoto and Gotemba to Sengokuhara. Having a rental car gives you maximum flexibility in this area — pick one up in Odawara or via pre-booking on Klook.

## Practical Tips for Staying in Hakone

- **Book ryokans well in advance.** The best ones sell out months ahead, especially during cherry blossom season (late March–April) and autumn foliage (November). Agoda often has last-minute deals, but don't count on it for top properties.
- **Get the Hakone Free Pass.** This covers unlimited rides on the Tozan Railway, ropeway, cable car, buses, and lake ferry for 2–3 days. Buy it at Shinjuku or Odawara. Excellent value if you're exploring multiple areas.
- **Check the Fuji visibility forecast.** Fuji is notoriously shy — summer is the cloudiest season, while winter mornings tend to offer the clearest views. Many ryokan websites post real-time Fuji visibility updates.
- **Ryokan etiquette matters.** Remove shoes at the entrance, fold your yukata left-over-right, don't bring towels into the communal baths, and keep noise low in shared spaces. Your fellow guests will appreciate it.
- **Meals are often included.** Most mid-range and upscale ryokans price rooms with dinner and breakfast (*ippaku nishoku*). Factor this into cost comparisons — the kaiseki dinners alone can be worth $80–$120 per person.
- **Allergies and dietary needs:** Tell your ryokan *before* arrival. Most can accommodate vegetarians or shellfish allergies with advance notice — Japanese hospitality is deeply accommodating.
