If you've spent more than five minutes scrolling through travel Instagram, you've almost certainly seen *those* photos — people wading through a mirrored infinity pool, surrounded by cascading digital flowers, looking like they've stepped into another dimension. That's teamLab Planets Tokyo, and yes, it's every bit as extraordinary in person as it looks online. But before you just show up and hope for the best, there's a fair bit of practical groundwork worth doing. ## What Is teamLab Planets Tokyo?

teamLab Planets is an immersive digital art museum created by the Japanese art collective **teamLab** — a group of over 1,000 creatives spanning artists, programmers, architects, engineers, and mathematicians. Unlike a traditional gallery where you stand at a respectful distance from a canvas, here you *become* part of the artwork. The installations respond to your movement, your touch, and the presence of other visitors around you.

Opened in 2018 in the Toyosu neighborhood, teamLab Planets was originally slated as a temporary exhibition but proved so wildly popular it became permanent. The venue spans roughly **10,000 square meters** across four main immersive rooms and two garden spaces. Plan on spending **60 to 90 minutes** inside — it's not massive, but it's dense with experience.

### How teamLab Planets Differs from teamLab Borderless

A common point of confusion: Tokyo has *two* major teamLab venues. **teamLab Borderless** (recently relocated to Azabudai Hills) is the larger, more labyrinthine experience with dozens of interconnected rooms. **teamLab Planets** is more intimate and focused, with fewer but arguably more intense installations — particularly its famous wading pool. If you only have time for one, Planets is generally the more emotionally impactful for first-timers.

## Tickets, Prices & How to Book

This is the most important section to pay attention to. **teamLab Planets does not sell tickets at the door.** All tickets must be purchased in advance online, and specific time slots sell out days — sometimes weeks — in advance during peak season.

### Current Ticket Prices (2025–2026)

| Ticket Type | Price (JPY) | Approx. USD |
|---|---|---|
| Adult (16+) | ¥3,200 | ~$21 |
| Junior (7–15) | ¥1,000 | ~$6.50 |
| Child (4–6) | ¥500 | ~$3.50 |
| Under 4 | Free | Free |

Prices may vary slightly on weekends and during special event periods. You can book directly on the [teamLab Planets official website](https://planets.teamlab.art/tokyo/), or grab your tickets through **Klook**, which is handy if you're bundling multiple Tokyo activities and want everything in one place — plus Klook sometimes offers small discounts on combo deals.

**Pro tip:** Book at least **1–2 weeks ahead** if you're visiting in spring (cherry blossom season) or around Golden Week (late April–early May). These slots disappear fast.

## Getting There: Address & Transport

**Address:** 6-1-16 Toyosu, Koto-ku, Tokyo 135-0061

teamLab Planets sits in the Toyosu waterfront district, which is easy to reach but not always on first-time visitors' radar.

### By Train (Recommended)
- Take the **Yurikamome Line** to **Shin-Toyosu Station** — the museum is literally a **3-minute walk** from the exit. This is the easiest option.
- Alternatively, take the **Tokyo Metro Yurakucho Line** to **Toyosu Station** (Exit 3), then walk about **10 minutes**.

### By Bus or Taxi
Taxis from Shibuya or Shinjuku will run you roughly ¥2,000–¥3,500 (~$13–$23) depending on traffic, which in Tokyo can be *very* unpredictable. The train is almost always faster and cheaper.

## What to Expect Inside: The Key Installations

Here's an honest walkthrough of what you'll actually encounter — no spoilers that ruin the magic, just enough so you're not caught off guard.

### The Wading Pool (Water Area)
This is the one everyone's seen online, and it absolutely delivers. You'll **remove your shoes and socks** before entering and wade through ankle-deep water filled with glowing, responsive digital koi and flowers. The projections respond to your footsteps in real time. It's genuinely breathtaking, especially when the room is at moderate capacity. If it's packed, the effect is slightly diminished — another reason to go at off-peak hours.

### Floating Flower Garden
You walk through a room where thousands of real and artificial flowers hang at eye level and above, slowly rising and falling around you. It smells incredible. This one hits differently if you go slowly and just... breathe.

### Infinite Crystal Universe
A forest of LED light sticks stretching in every direction, creating infinite reflections. You can interact with the display via your smartphone. It's dazzling and weirdly meditative.

### Moss Garden of Resonating Microcosms
Part of the outdoor garden section, this installation features illuminated orbs that change color and "resonate" when touched — each orb affecting the ones around it. It's especially beautiful after dark.

### The Hammock Area
A quieter moment in the experience — suspended nets you can lie back in while color and light wash over the ceiling above you. One of the most underrated spots in the entire venue.

## Best Time to Visit

teamLab Planets is open **daily**, generally from **10:00 AM to 10:00 PM** (last entry around 9:00 PM), though hours can shift seasonally — always check the official site before you go.

**Weekday mornings** (opening time through about noon) are consistently the least crowded. **Weekend evenings** are the most packed. The wading pool and mirror rooms in particular suffer when overcrowded — the magic is real but fragile.

If you're staying near Shibuya or Shinjuku, booking a **hotel on Agoda** in that area gives you easy access to the Yurikamome Line and keeps you central for the rest of your Tokyo itinerary.

## Practical Tips Before You Go

A few things that will genuinely make your visit better:

- **Wear shorts or roll up your pants.** You will get wet in the wading pool. There's no getting around it. Lockers are available for bags and shoes.
- **Skirts are fine** — the floor is mirrored in some areas, and the venue thoughtfully provides shorts to wear underneath. They'll hand them to you at the entrance.
- **Photography is encouraged and expected.** No flash needed; the rooms are luminous. Portrait mode on your phone works brilliantly.
- **Arrive 10–15 minutes before your time slot.** There's a brief check-in process and you'll want time to stow your belongings.
- **Go slow.** Many visitors rush through in 45 minutes. The people who get the most out of it are the ones who sit, breathe, and let the installations respond to them.
- **Avoid school holiday weeks** (late July through August, late December–early January) if you're crowd-sensitive. These are the busiest periods by far.
- **The outdoor garden section is worth lingering in**, especially if you visit in the evening — it's often skipped by people who rush the interior rooms.

## Is teamLab Planets Worth It?

Honestly? For most visitors, yes — emphatically. At around **$21 USD**, it's one of the more reasonably priced "bucket list" experiences in Tokyo compared to what it delivers. The combination of technology, nature, and genuine interactivity creates something you won't find anywhere else in quite the same way. It's not a quick photo op — it's an experience that tends to make people reflective, joyful, and a little bit speechless.

If you're a skeptic who finds "immersive digital art" a bit gimmicky, teamLab Planets may just be the thing that changes your mind. Go in with an open mind, take your time, and let the koi swim around your feet.
