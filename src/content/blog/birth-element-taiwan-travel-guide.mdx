## What Is Saju — And Why Should Travelers Care?

Saju (사주), sometimes called the *Four Pillars of Destiny*, is a Korean astrological system rooted in ancient Chinese metaphysics. While Western astrology relies on your sun sign alone, Saju calculates four distinct pillars based on your **exact birth year, month, day, and hour** — each governed by one of the Five Elements: **Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water**.

Think of it as the Eastern alternative to the Western zodiac, but with significantly more nuance. Your dominant birth element doesn't just reveal personality traits — it points toward the environments, textures, landscapes, and energies where you'll *thrive*. And that makes it a surprisingly practical travel planning tool.

Taiwan, with its extraordinary diversity — from misty mountain forests and volcanic hot springs to neon-drenched night markets and serene coastal highways — happens to be one of the most element-rich destinations in all of Asia. There's a corner of this island for every birth element. Let's find yours.

## Wood Element: Alishan & the Forest Railways of the Central Mountains

If Wood is your dominant element, you are drawn to growth, nature, living systems, and wide open green spaces. You recharge in forests. You probably already hike more than most of your friends.

**Head straight to Alishan National Scenic Area** in Chiayi County.

### What to Do

- Ride the **Alishan Forest Railway** — one of the few surviving mountain railways of its kind in Asia. A round-trip ticket costs around **$8–12 USD** and the views are genuinely cinematic.
- Wake before dawn for the famous **sunrise at Zhushan Viewing Platform**. The sea of clouds rolling beneath the peaks is the kind of sight that recalibrates your nervous system.
- Walk the **Giant Tree Trail** among thousand-year-old red cypresses — some over 45 meters tall and 2,000 years old.

### Where to Stay

The **Alishan House** (竹迹館) sits right within the forest reserve. Rooms start around **$90–130 USD/night** — book via Agoda for early-bird rates, especially during cherry blossom season (late February to March) when the place fills up weeks in advance.

**Transport:** Take the high-speed rail (HSR) to Chiayi Station (~$35 USD from Taipei), then a local bus or taxi to Alishan (~$8 USD, 2.5 hours).

## Fire Element: Taipei's Night Markets & Urban Energy

Fire types crave stimulation, connection, and the electric buzz of human energy. Quiet mountaintop retreats sound lovely in theory — but by day two you'd be restless. You need *movement*, color, and conversation.

**Taipei is your city.**

### What to Do

- **Shilin Night Market** is the most famous for a reason — arrive after 7pm and lose yourself in stinky tofu, oyster omelettes, and sugar-coated strawberries. Budget **$10–20 USD** for a full evening of eating.
- Climb **Elephant Mountain (Xiangshan)** at sunset for the iconic Taipei 101 skyline shot — it's free and about 20 minutes of hiking.
- Book a **Taipei street food night market tour** on Klook (from ~$35 USD) if you want an English-speaking guide who knows exactly which stalls are worth the queue.
- Explore the **Ximending** district for vintage fashion, indie music, and the kind of spontaneous encounters that Fire types live for.

### Where to Stay

Stay in the **Da'an or Zhongzheng districts** to be within walking distance of MRT lines. Mid-range options on Agoda like the **Just Sleep Ximending** run **$60–90 USD/night** with great transport access.

## Earth Element: Jiufen, Tainan & the Slow Pleasures of Heritage Taiwan

Earth types are grounded, sensory, and deeply appreciative of history, craftsmanship, and places that feel *rooted*. You don't rush. You want to understand a place, not just pass through it.

**You'll fall completely in love with two very different corners of Taiwan.**

### Jiufen (Northern Taiwan)

Perched on a hillside overlooking the Pacific, **Jiufen** is a former gold-mining village that feels suspended in time. Its red lantern-lit teahouses, narrow stone staircases, and misty harbor views famously inspired the aesthetic of *Spirited Away*.

- Spend an afternoon at **Jiufen Old Street** — try taro balls (*芋圓*, around **$2–3 USD** a bowl) at A-Zhu Taro Ball Shop.
- Take the **Gold Ecological Park tour** from nearby Jinguashi (Klook offers day tours from Taipei including transport for ~$40 USD).

### Tainan (Southern Taiwan)

Taiwan's oldest city, **Tainan**, is the spiritual and culinary heartland of the island. Earth elements will want *at least* three days here.

- Visit the **Chihkan Tower** and **Anping Fort** — both under $3 USD entry.
- Eat your way through **Tainan's breakfast culture**: beef soup (*牛肉湯*) at 6am, milkfish congee, and coffin bread are non-negotiables.

**Transport to Tainan:** HSR from Taipei takes about 90 minutes (~$30 USD). The city is best explored by bicycle — rentals via YouBike cost around **$0.30 USD per 30 minutes**.

## Metal Element: Taroko Gorge & the Dramatic East Coast

Metal types are precise, resilient, and drawn to the sublime — landscapes that feel *vast*, *structured*, and slightly awe-inspiring. You want to feel small in the best possible way.

**Taroko Gorge in Hualien County is your element manifest in marble and river.**

### What to Do

- Walk the **Shakadang Trail** along the turquoise Liwu River — free to enter, 4.4km return, and the water color is genuinely unreal.
- Hike to **Baiyang Waterfall** (permit required, apply free via the Taroko National Park website at least 1–2 weeks ahead).
- Book a **full-day Taroko Gorge cycling and hiking tour** on Klook (~$55–75 USD including transport and guide) — Metal types will appreciate the structured itinerary.

### Where to Stay

**Silks Place Taroko** is the standout hotel here — rooms from ~$180 USD/night on Agoda, right at the gorge entrance with mountain views from every room.

**Transport:** Train from Taipei to Hualien takes ~2 hours (~$15 USD on the Puyuma Express). Book train tickets on the Taiwan Railways website at least a week ahead.

## Water Element: Sun Moon Lake & the Hot Springs of Beitou

Water types are intuitive, reflective, and deeply restorative by nature. You travel to *decompress*, not to tick off sights. You need stillness, depth, and somewhere to just *be*.

Two destinations were practically made for you.

### Sun Moon Lake (Nantou County)

Taiwan's largest lake sits at 748 meters altitude surrounded by forested hills. The light here changes every hour — at dawn it's ethereal, at dusk it's pure cinema.

- Cycle the **33km Sun Moon Lake cycling path** (bike rental ~$8 USD/day) at your own pace.
- Take a **boat cruise** across the lake to the Xuanguang Temple (~$6 USD round-trip).
- Stay at the **Lalu Sun Moon Lake** — a luxury lakeside resort from ~$220 USD/night on Agoda, and worth every cent for a Water element who needs genuine restoration.

### Beitou Hot Springs (Taipei)

If you're short on time, **Beitou** is just 30 minutes from central Taipei on the MRT (free with EasyCard transfer). The **Xinbeitou Hot Spring Museum** is free to visit, and public baths at spots like **Millennium Hot Spring** cost just **$2 USD** for a two-hour soak.

### General Taiwan Travel Tips

- **Get an EasyCard** (悠遊卡) at any 7-Eleven or airport MRT station (~$5 USD deposit) — it works on MRT, buses, YouBike, and even some convenience stores.
- **Taiwan's healthcare is excellent** but travel insurance is still strongly recommended. World Nomads covers most adventure activities including hiking.
- **Google Maps works perfectly** in Taiwan. Download offline maps for mountain areas where signal can drop.
- **Budget ~$25–40 USD/day** for food if you eat like a local — night markets and local lunch spots are incredible value.
- Taiwan is generally very safe for solo travelers of all genders.
