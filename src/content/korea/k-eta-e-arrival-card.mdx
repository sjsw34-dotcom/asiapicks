## Which form do you need?

One of the two, and for most visitors it is the e-Arrival Card. The rule is simpler than the coverage suggests: if you are going through the K-ETA process you file that and nothing else, and if you are not, you file the e-Arrival Card instead.

Because nationals of eligible visa-free countries are currently exempt from the K-ETA, most visitors fall into the second group. Exemption moves the paperwork rather than removing it.

## Why does "no K-ETA needed" mislead people?

Because it is reported as good news about paperwork, and it is actually news about which paperwork. The K-ETA exemption for eligible visa-free nationals runs until 31 December 2026. Read quickly, that sounds like nothing to do before flying. It is the opposite: not holding a K-ETA is the precise condition that makes the e-Arrival Card mandatory for you.

KPMG's Global Mobility briefing of 30 January 2026 puts it plainly. The e-Arrival Card is required for any foreign traveller not subject to the K-ETA application process, and travellers who are exempt may apply for a K-ETA voluntarily to gain benefits including exemption from submitting an e-Arrival Card.

So the two forms are alternatives. What you cannot do is treat the exemption as covering both.

## What happened to the paper arrival card?

It is gone. South Korea withdrew the paper arrival card on 1 January 2026 and replaced it with the digital e-Arrival Card. Any guide describing a card handed out by cabin crew on approach, or a desk to fill one in after landing, is describing a system that no longer runs.

This is the single most common way current advice about Korean entry is wrong, and it is worth using as a freshness test. A page that still explains how to complete the paper card has not been checked since 2025.

## When is the e-Arrival Card due?

In the three days before you arrive. The card is submitted ahead of landing rather than after it, and it is free.

The practical reading is that this belongs with the last checks before a flight rather than with the things booked months ahead. The window opens three days out, so it cannot be completed early the way a K-ETA can, and it cannot be left for the airport either: the desk that used to hand out paper cards is gone.

## Should you apply for a K-ETA anyway?

There is one concrete reason to: it exempts you from the e-Arrival Card. If you would rather complete one authorisation well in advance than a form in the days before departure, applying voluntarily achieves that.

Our pick for most short trips is the simpler path of filing the e-Arrival Card, because it is free and quick and the exemption already saves you the K-ETA. Anyone travelling repeatedly, or who dislikes having a deadline in the week of the flight, has the better case for applying.

## What to check before you book

Two things change, and both have changed recently. The list of countries eligible for the exemption is published on the official K-ETA site and is the only authority on whether it covers you. And the exemption has an explicit end date, 31 December 2026, which has been extended a year at a time rather than made permanent. Anyone travelling in 2027 should assume the K-ETA is back until an extension is announced.

The other task for the week of the flight is data. A tourist eSIM with a Korean number can be bought before you leave and switched on at the airport, and [which Korea eSIM to get](/korea/korea-esim-with-phone-number) compares the three carriers.
