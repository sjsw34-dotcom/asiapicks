## When is the Busan Fireworks Festival 2026?

On Saturday 7 November 2026. The Busan Festival Organizing Committee, which runs the festival for Busan Metropolitan City, lists it as the 21st edition, staged across Gwangalli Beach, Igidae and Dongbaekseom.

The official ticket listing gives the show as 7pm to 8pm, with an intermission scheduled from 7:15pm to 7:25pm. Fireworks are fired from the sea in front of Gwangan Bridge, so everything worth seeing faces the bridge.

<Figure id="busan-fireworks-festival" />

The festival is one night only, so plan the rest of the trip around 7 November rather than the other way round.

## How much are the paid seats?

R seats cost 100,000 won and come with a table and chairs. S seats cost 70,000 won and are chairs only. Both sit in a fenced block on the sand in front of Hotel Aquapellis and Homers Hotel, according to Seoul Shinmun and BusinessKorea's reports on the 4 September launch.

Busan residents get 10% off, at 90,000 and 63,000 won. The discount needs proof of a Busan address at the on-site ticket booth, so it does not apply to visitors. Foreign residents qualify only with an Alien Registration Card or residence report card showing a Busan address.

Demand is real. Seoul Shinmun reported that the early-bird allocation released on 26 August sold out in about a minute.

## How do foreign visitors buy tickets?

Book on NOL World, the English site of the official ticket seller. The festival's English site links there, and the NOL World festival page says it is for international visitors only, with passport verification at booking.

Each person can buy up to 4 regular tickets. Sales run until 5pm on Friday 6 November 2026, and the Korean listing allows cancellation until 5pm the day before the show.

Collection depends on when you book. NOL World says bookings completed by 11 October are delivered in advance from 12 October, and bookings made from 12 October are collected on site. Someone else can collect for you with a copy of the buyer's ID and the booking confirmation.

The English site also sells hotel packages through NOL World. Compare the package price with a separate seat and room before paying for the bundle.

<Callout type="tip">
Seat holders can enter from 3pm. NOL World states that if you do not reach your assigned seat by 6pm, you will not be let in and the ticket will not be refunded.
</Callout>

## Where can you watch for free?

Anywhere outside the paid block. The rest of Gwangalli Beach is open, and the Korea Tourism Organization names Hwangnyeongsan, Dongbaekseom and Igidae Coastal Park as alternatives to the beach.

**Gwangalli Beach, outside the paid block.** Closest to the barges and the loudest. Spots on the sand fill hours before the show, and you will stand or sit on the ground in a very dense crowd.

**Igidae Coastal Park.** One of the organisers' three official venues, a coastal park across the water from the beach. Expect coastal paths rather than a promenade, and bring a light for the walk out.

**Dongbaekseom.** Another official venue, on the Haeundae side of the bay. It suits anyone staying in Haeundae.

**Hwangnyeongsan.** A viewpoint from above rather than from the shore. A high angle takes in the whole bridge, the barges and the city at once, like the photo at the top of this page, at the cost of distance.

**Our pick** for a first-timer who will not pay for a seat is Dongbaekseom. It is an official venue and it keeps you away from the beach where the densest crowd and the road closures are.

## How do you get there and back?

By subway, and early. The organisers ask visitors to use public transport, provide no parking near the venue, and close the roads around Gwangalli in stages from 10am on Saturday 7 November, so taxis and cars are the slow option.

For Gwangalli Beach, the festival's directions page sends visitors to Gwangan Station on Line 2, Exit 3. Visit Busan puts the beach a 13-minute walk from Exit 5. For the other venues, the organisers name Dongbaek Station (Exit 1) on Line 2 for Dongbaekseom, and Kyungsung Univ. and Pukyong Nat'l Univ. Station on Line 2, then a bus, for Igidae.

Plan the way back before you go. The whole crowd leaves at about 8pm through the same few stations, and a station you arrived at can be skipped on the way out. Walking one or two stations away from the beach before boarding is the simplest fix.

### Is there a 2026 subway plan?

Not yet. Busan Transportation Corporation (Humetro) had not published its 2026 subway plan for 7 November when we checked on 29 October 2026, so extra trains, skipped stations, closed exits and any later last train are still unconfirmed. Busan city's safety committee reviewed public transport for the festival on 27 October 2026, but its press release gives no train figures.

Last year's pattern, for the 2025 festival on 15 November, is the best guide until the 2026 plan appears:

- Humetro ran 200 extra trains on Lines 1 to 3, cutting the wait to 4.5 to 6 minutes between 4pm and 7pm and to 3 to 5 minutes between 8pm and 10pm
- it asked people to go home from Suyeong, Geumnyeonsan or Namcheon stations instead of Gwangan
- when stations got crowded it held passengers back in stages, first outside the entrances, then at the ticket gates, then at the platforms
- Busan Ilbo reported that empty trains were sent into Suyeong, Gwangan and Geumnyeonsan stations when they filled up, with a warning that some stations could be skipped, and that buses on 21 routes near the venue were added, some running until midnight

### Which roads close on 7 November?

The roads behind Gwangalli Beach close to traffic in stages from 10am, and every closure stays in place until the event ends. The organisers' 2026 traffic plan sets these stages:

| From | Closed to traffic until the event ends |
|---|---|
| 10am | Gwanganhaebyeon-ro along the beach, Eonyang junction to Mannam Square (0.82km) |
| 2pm | Hwangnyeongsan-ro, Kyungdong Construction head office entrance to the Hwangnyeongsan beacon (2km), possibly earlier if crowds build |
| 4pm | Haebyeon-ro, Mannam Square to the junction in front of Millak Park (0.8km); the one-way back street between Haebyeon-ro and Gwangnam-ro (1.5km); Marine City 1-ro (1.1km); the Hwangnyeongsan loop road (5.5km) |
| 5pm | Gwangnam-ro, KBS junction to Millak Bridge (3.3km); Millaksubyeon-ro; further stretches of Haebyeon-ro; Igidae Park road one-way only, or closed if traffic is heavy. Buses still run at this stage |
| 6pm | Gwangnam-ro closed to buses as well, affecting 11 routes (9 regular, 2 village buses) |
| 7:30pm | Gwangan-ro, Gwangan Station to the beach junction (0.48km), and Namcheonbada-ro, Geumnyeonsan Station to Eonyang junction (0.2km) |

Suyeong-ro is the official detour for most of these roads. The organisers add that closures may be adjusted on the day to match traffic.

### When is Gwangan Bridge closed?

Completely on Saturday 7 November 2026: the upper deck, which carries traffic from Haeundae towards Nam-gu, from 3:30pm to midnight, and the lower deck, from Nam-gu towards Haeundae, from 6:30pm to midnight.

The bridge also loses lanes during the week around the festival:

- upper deck: one lane closed from 8pm to 6am each night from 2 to 4 November, and from 8pm on 5 November to 9am on 6 November, then two lanes from 9am on 6 November until the full closure at 3:30pm on 7 November
- lower deck: one lane closed from 9am on 4 November until the full closure at 6:30pm on 7 November, then again from midnight to 6pm on 8 November

Allow extra time that week for any taxi or transfer that would normally cross the bridge between Haeundae and the south and west of the city.

## Where should you stay for the fireworks?

In Gwangalli if you can get a room, ideally one facing the bridge. Gwangalli is the base our Busan accommodation guide suggests to anyone undecided, and on this one night it puts you within walking distance of the show, which removes the transport problem entirely.

A bridge-view room is the only way to watch in comfort without a paid seat. Book as soon as your dates are fixed, because every room on the beachfront is competing for the same Saturday.

## How does the festival fit into a Busan trip?

Build the trip around the Saturday night, and keep that afternoon free. With seat entry from 3pm, or a free spot claimed in the early afternoon, the festival takes most of the day.

The day before or after suits the rest of the city: the old port around Busan Station on one day, the east coast from Haeundae on the other.
