## What is Gamcheon Culture Village?

Gamcheon is a residential neighbourhood on a hillside in Saha-gu, western Busan, not a theme park. Saha-gu describes it as a settlement formed in the 1950s by followers of the Taegeukdo religion and by refugees from the Korean War. The houses climb the slope in steps, which the village residents' council explains as a layout that keeps each house from blocking the sun and view of the one behind.

The colour came later. In 2009 a Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism village art project brought students, artists and residents together to paint the lanes and fill them with artworks, according to Visit Busan and the residents' council. Kookje Shinmun reports that the village drew about 3.12 million visitors in 2025.

Those numbers are why the rules below exist. People live in most of the houses you walk past, and the lanes were built for them, not for coach parties.

## What are the visiting hours?

Between 9am and 6pm. Saha-gu made the village a special management zone on 1 August 2026, and in its dense residential area tourists are asked to visit only within those hours, so that residents have quiet evenings, as Kookje Shinmun reported and the Korea Tourism Organization summarises in English.

The rule is being phased in. Saha-gu is running it as a trial from August 2026 and plans full enforcement from 1 January 2027, with 35 village wardens on the ground handling guidance, traffic and order. The lanes that mix homes and shops are managed with etiquette guidance rather than hours.

The information centre at the entrance is open from 9am to 6pm every day, according to the village's own site. Visit Busan gives a 5pm close from November to February, so on a winter afternoon plan to finish earlier.

## Is it free, and do you need a map?

Entry is free; Visit Busan's listing charges only for parking. The information centre sells an alley tour map and keeps luggage, according to the residents' council.

Since August 2026, groups of 10 or more must buy a map, one each, from the information centre or the village website. For a pair or a family, the map is optional, though it is how the stamp course below is laid out.

## How do you get to Gamcheon Culture Village?

By subway and a village bus. Take Line 1 to Toseong Station and leave by exit 6. Village buses Saha 1-1, Seo-gu 2 and Seo-gu 2-2 climb to the Gamjeong Elementary School stop, a five-minute walk from the village, according to Visit Busan.

The residents' council gives a second boarding point for the same buses, at the Chungmu Market stop by exit 1 of Jagalchi Station, which suits a morning at the fish market first. From the other side, village buses Saha 1 and Saha 1-1 run from exit 6 of Goejeong Station on the same line.

The Busan City Tour's Orange Line also stops at Gamcheon, at the Gamcheon intersection, on its loop from Wednesday to Sunday, with departures listed at 10am, 11:20am, 12:40pm, 2pm, 3:20pm and 4:40pm.

## Can you drive or take a taxi?

Not along the village's main street in the daytime. Saha-gu restricts vehicles carrying tourists, taxis included, on part of Gamnae 2-ro from 9am to 6pm, for pedestrians' safety, and the Korea Tourism Organization says large buses need a reservation. Fines for that restriction start on 1 January 2028; until then wardens enforce it with guidance.

Parking is short anyway. The residents' council lists two public car parks, with 78 and 40 spaces, and asks visitors to come by public transport. The village bus is the simplest way up.

## What is a good walking route?

Saha-gu publishes two. The main street there and back takes about 40 minutes. The stamp course takes about 1 hour 20 minutes and runs from the information centre past the Small Museum, the Haneul Maru viewpoint and the Little Prince, then down through the lanes.

The stops on it are small. The Small Museum, by the entrance, shows residents' old photographs and household objects and tells the story of the art project. Haneul Maru, a preserved house turned into a viewpoint and exhibition room, looks out over Busan Port and Gamcheon Port, according to the residents' council.

The Little Prince is the photo most visitors come for: a sculpture of the prince and the desert fox looking out over the village, which Visit Busan calls the highlight. Our pick is to arrive soon after 9am, walk the stamp course first and leave the main street's cafés for the way back.

## What etiquette does the village ask for?

Treat it as a street where people live. The Korea Tourism Organization's summary of the zone's rules asks visitors not to talk loudly, not to look inside houses or take photos of them without permission, and to take rubbish away or use the bins provided. Visit Busan adds that drones are not allowed.

Seoul's Bukchon has the same kind of zone, with the difference that Bukchon fines tourists who stay in its red zone after hours; Gamcheon relies on wardens. The [Bukchon Hanok Village guide](/korea/seoul/bukchon-hanok-village) explains how that works.

## How does Gamcheon fit into a day in Busan?

It belongs with the old port. Toseong and Jagalchi are both on Line 1 with Busan Station, Nampo and the Bupyeong night market, so Gamcheon fits the morning of the old-port day in the [Busan 2-day itinerary](/korea/busan/busan-2-day-itinerary), before Gukje Market and the night market.
