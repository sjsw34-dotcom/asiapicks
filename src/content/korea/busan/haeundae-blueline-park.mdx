## What is Haeundae Blueline Park?

Haeundae Blueline Park is a pair of rides on a disused coastal railway that runs east from Haeundae Beach. The Beach Train runs 4.8km along the old line from Mipo through Cheongsapo to Songjeong, and the Sky Capsule runs on an elevated track above it for 2km between Mipo and Cheongsapo, according to Haeundae Blueline Park.

Mipo, the starting point, sits at the eastern end of Haeundae Beach, as Visit Busan describes it. A walking trail, the Busan Green Railway, runs beside the track the whole way from Mipo to Songjeong, and walking it costs nothing; only the rides are paid.

## Should you ride the Sky Capsule or the Beach Train?

Ride the Sky Capsule if you are two to four people and want the view to yourselves; take the Beach Train if you are alone, want to go past Cheongsapo, or are deciding on the day. Our pick for a pair or a small group is the capsule one way from Mipo to Cheongsapo and the Beach Train back.

The capsule is a small private car for up to four people, 7 to 10 metres above the shore, moving at about 4km per hour, so the 2km take about 30 minutes. Blueline Park says the capsule has the same space whether two, three or four people ride, and the fare is set by the head count. It only runs one way per ticket, between Mipo and Cheongsapo.

The Beach Train is the transport half. It runs at about 15km per hour, three trips an hour, and Visit Busan notes that its seats face the sea. Seats are not assigned, and you can stand. It is the only one of the two that reaches Songjeong.

For a solo traveller the capsule is poor value: the fare table starts at two people, and Blueline Park's English pages list no single-rider fare. The Beach Train is the obvious choice there.

## How much do the tickets cost?

The Sky Capsule is priced per capsule, one way: {{blueline-sky-capsule-two}} for two people, 55,000 won for three and 60,000 won for four. The Beach Train is priced per person: {{blueline-beach-train-one-way-end}} for one ride from Mipo or Songjeong, {{blueline-beach-train-one-way-cheongsapo}} from Cheongsapo or one of the smaller stops, 14,000 won for a two-ride pass and 16,000 won for an all-stops day pass. These are the fares that took effect on 1 May 2026.

The ticket rules matter as much as the prices. A one-ride Beach Train ticket ends when you get off, with no re-boarding. The two-ride pass lets you board at two stations of your choice. The all-stops pass lets you board once at each station, but not twice at the same one. Everyone must get off at Mipo and Songjeong, the end stations, so a return trip needs a two-ride pass or two tickets.

There is also a package: a one-way capsule plus an all-stops Beach Train pass for each rider, at 73,000 won for two, 92,000 won for three and 111,000 won for four. It suits a day of hopping off along the line to Songjeong. If you only want the capsule out and the train back, buying them separately is cheaper: for two, the capsule plus two single Beach Train tickets from Cheongsapo comes to 66,000 won.

Infants under 36 months ride free on both. A group of five may share one capsule on the four-person fare only when one of them is a child under seven (born in 2020 or later); otherwise five people need two capsules. Discounts for Busan residents are sold only at the ticket window, with ID.

## What are the operating hours?

In October the Sky Capsule runs from 8:30am to 6:30pm and the Beach Train from 9am to 7:30pm. From November to February the capsule runs from 8:30am to 6pm and the Beach Train from 9am to 7pm, from both Mipo and Songjeong.

On Saturday 7 November 2026, the night of the [Busan Fireworks Festival](/korea/busan/busan-fireworks-festival), both rides run late: the last capsule entry is 7pm to 7:30pm and the last Beach Train leaves Songjeong at 8pm and Mipo at 8:30pm.

## Where are the stations?

There are three staffed stations. Mipo, at the Haeundae end, has the Beach Train on the ground floor and the Sky Capsule on the second floor. Cheongsapo, in the middle, is the other end of the capsule line and a Beach Train stop. Songjeong, at the far end, serves the Beach Train only.

Between them the Beach Train also stops at Dalmaji Tunnel, the Haewol skywalk, the Daritdol skywalk above Cheongsapo, and Gudeokpo. Those smaller stops have ticket machines but no ticket office.

All three main stations have lockers. Suitcases are allowed on both rides, but Blueline Park recommends the lockers because space is tight. The lockers take only 1,000 won notes and charge extra after midnight.

## How do you book, and how far ahead?

Book on Blueline Park's English site. Tickets for the week four weeks ahead, Monday to Sunday, go on sale every Tuesday at 7am. On the day, online sales only cover departures from two hours after the time you book, so book at least the day before for a set time.

1. Choose the ride and the station you will board at; you can only board where you booked.
2. Pick a time slot. For the capsule, the slot is an entry window (an 11am to 11:30am slot means you enter between those times and board in order), not the departure time.
3. Enter your details carefully: the mobile ticket arrives by KakaoTalk or email, and its QR code is the boarding pass.
4. Arrive early. Beach Train boarding closes 10 minutes before departure, and anyone more than 30 minutes late for either ride may be refused, without a refund.

Blueline Park's own FAQ warns that some overseas credit cards are declined because of its card authentication method. Its list of official ticket vendors includes Trip.com, Klook and KKday, which are the fallback if your card fails.

On-site tickets exist but are limited, first come, first served, and can sell out early. Early boarding is not allowed on the capsule, and you cannot change a booking's time: cancel and rebook.

Cancelling three or more days before the ride gets a full refund, two days before 80%, one day before 70%, and on the day 50%. Once the boarding time has passed, there is no refund.

## What happens in rain or a typhoon?

Both rides run normally in rain. Blueline Park says operation may stop in a natural disaster such as heavy rain or a typhoon, and that emergency safety inspections can change the hours without notice. Check the notices on the official site on the morning of your ride if the forecast is bad.

## How do you get to Mipo, Cheongsapo and Songjeong?

For Mipo, take Line 2 to Jung-dong, the stop after Haeundae, and walk about 15 minutes from exit 7, roughly 800 metres. Buses 39, 100, 141 and 200 from exit 7 of Haeundae Station stop at the Mipo and Moontan Road entrance, about 10 minutes' walk from the station.

For Songjeong, take the Donghae Line to Songjeong Station and walk about 12 minutes from exit 2. Buses including 39, 100, 139 and 181 stop at Songjeong Complex 1 nearby.

For Cheongsapo, take Haeundae-gu village bus 2 to the Blueline Park stop, or village bus 10 to the stop in front of the supermarket, from exit 5 of Jangsan Station or exit 1 of Haeundae Station.

## Can you combine it with Haedong Yonggungsa?

Yes, and the Beach Train's far end makes it easy. Ride the train to Songjeong, walk about 12 minutes to Songjeong Station, take the Donghae Line one stop to OSIRIA, and from exit 1 take bus 139 to the Haedong Yonggungsa Temple stop, the route Visit Busan gives for the temple.

The temple is free and open every day from 4:30am to 7:20pm, with last entry at 6:50pm, so the order can go either way. The [Busan 2-day itinerary](/korea/busan/busan-2-day-itinerary) puts the temple first, early, and the capsule after it, then the afternoon on Haeundae Beach.

Haeundae, if the coast is the point of the trip: Mipo is at the east end of Haeundae Beach, so the rides start inside the district.
