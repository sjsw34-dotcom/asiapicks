## How do you get there?

You fly. Jeju is an island off the southern coast and the KTX does not reach it, which makes it the one major Korean destination that cannot be folded into a rail itinerary. Gimpo to Jeju is the shortest and most frequent hop from Seoul, and it runs often enough that people treat it more like a shuttle than a flight.

That single fact changes how Jeju fits a trip. Busan and Gyeongju sit on a line you can travel along; Jeju is a separate leg you commit to. It rewards three nights and punishes one.

## What did UNESCO actually list?

Jeju Volcanic Island and Lava Tubes was inscribed as a World Natural Heritage site in 2007. The Korea Heritage Service names three components: the Mount Halla Natural Reserve, the Geomunoreum Lava Tube System and Seongsan Sunrise Peak.

Worth reading that list carefully, because it is not the whole island. The listing is volcanic: a mountain, a cave system and a tuff cone. Plenty of what visitors come for, the beaches, the cafés, the coastal walking, sits outside it, and plenty of the listing is not casually visitable.

## Do you need a car?

This is the decision that shapes the whole trip, and it deserves deciding before you book anything. Jeju is a ring. The sights are spread around the coastal edge rather than gathered in a centre, so the island rewards driving in a way that Seoul actively punishes it.

Without a car the island is still workable, but it becomes a different holiday: fewer bases, more time on bus routes, days built around one area rather than three stops. That is not a lesser trip, and for anyone who does not want to drive on an unfamiliar island it is the honest choice. It is simply a different one, and guides that treat the car as optional detail get this backwards.

Our pick for a first visit is three nights, and deciding the car question first. [Jeju without a car](/korea/jeju/jeju-without-a-car) sets out what the island still gives you if the answer is no.

## When should you go?

Jeju sits south of the mainland and runs milder, which makes the shoulder seasons longer here than in Seoul. Spring and autumn are the reliable windows, as they are everywhere in Korea, but Jeju holds its season a little later into November and starts a little earlier in March.

Summer brings both the heat and the crowds, because the island is where Koreans go on holiday too. That is worth knowing when you price accommodation for July and August.
