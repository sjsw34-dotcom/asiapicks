## Does it work without a car?

Yes. Jeju has an island-wide bus network and the sights people come for are served by it. Nobody is stranded.

What changes is not the quality of the trip but its shape. Jeju is a ring with no centre, so a car lets you treat the island as a list to work through, while buses make you treat it as a place to choose a part of. Both are good holidays. Only one of them fits the itinerary most people arrive with.

## Why the geography matters more here

On the mainland the question of a car is usually about convenience. On Jeju it is about coverage.

The island's draws are distributed around the coastal edge and across the interior: the crater at Seongsan in the east, the waterfalls and cliffs in the south, Hallasan in the middle, the quieter west. There is no hub they all radiate from, so moving between two of them in a day means crossing a meaningful share of the island.

With a car that is an hour. On buses it is the afternoon. Neither is a problem until you have planned a day that assumes the first and buys the second.

## How to plan a car-free trip

Pick a side and stay on it. This is the whole technique, and it is the opposite of what most Jeju itineraries suggest.

Basing yourself in the east puts Seongsan and the surrounding coast within short hops. Basing yourself in the south near Seogwipo does the same for the waterfalls and cliffs. Either produces full days with little transit. Trying to do both from one base produces two half-days and a lot of bus.

For trips beyond three nights, two bases beat one. Move once, deliberately, rather than commuting daily across the island.

## What you gain

One thing, and it is not a consolation prize. The Olle trail is a network of waymarked coastal walking routes, and it is the part of Jeju a car actively gets in the way of: you cannot drive a walking route, and the logistics of returning to a parked car shape the day around the vehicle rather than the coast.

Travelling by bus makes point-to-point walking natural, because you do not need to end where you began. Our pick for a car-free trip is to build at least one full day around a single Olle section and treat it as the day rather than a gap between sights.

## Decide it before you book

This is the practical point of the whole article. The car question determines where you should stay, and accommodation is what people book first.

Book a car and a single central base works. Go car-free and the same base produces a week of long bus rides. Deciding the transport after the hotel is how car-free Jeju trips get their reputation for being difficult, and the difficulty is in the sequence rather than the island.
