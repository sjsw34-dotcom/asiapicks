## Which station, and what is it called now?

Gyeongju Station. If that sounds obvious, it is not: the KTX station was called Singyeongju, meaning new Gyeongju, from its opening in 2010 until 28 December 2023, when Korail completed the rename following Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport notice 2023-45.

Plenty of guides, forum answers and older booking pages still tell you to get off at Singyeongju. They are describing the same platform. The confusion is worse than it sounds, because the city also had a separate older Gyeongju station on the conventional line, so a visitor reading two sources can reasonably conclude there are two places to choose between.

The station also sits outside the centre, so arriving is not the same as being there. Plan the local bus or taxi leg rather than assuming you step off the train into the old city.

## What did a thousand years leave behind?

Gyeongju was the capital of the Silla kingdom for nearly a millennium, and what makes it unusual is the distribution. The remains are not fenced into one archaeological park; they sit among streets, schools and shops. Burial mounds the size of small hills rise out of ordinary blocks.

UNESCO inscribed Seokguram Grotto and Bulguksa Temple in 1995, and the Gyeongju Historic Areas in 2000, which is a rare density of listing for one city.

## Is a day trip enough?

A day trip is how most people see Gyeongju, and it covers Bulguksa plus one or two central sites. That is not nothing. It is also roughly the fraction of the city that fits between two train rides, which is a different claim from having seen the place.

The argument for a night is the distribution problem above. Gyeongju's sights are scattered, several are best at either end of the day, and a day trip spends its daylight on the two that are easiest to reach. Our pick is one night, arriving in the afternoon, which buys the evening at Donggung and Wolji and a full morning for Bulguksa before the coaches. [Where to stay in Gyeongju](/korea/gyeongju/where-to-stay-in-gyeongju) compares the old centre, Bomun and Bulguksa for that night.

It also pairs naturally with Busan, an hour further down the same line, and guided day trips to Gyeongju leave from Busan for anyone basing themselves there instead.
