**Our pick** is the old centre around Hwangnidan-gil and Daereungwon. The rest of this guide explains why, and when one of the others wins.

## Why does the area matter so much in Gyeongju?

Because Gyeongju has no subway and its sights sit in three separate places. The tombs, Cheomseongdae and Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond are in the old centre. Bomun Lake, with the large hotels, is about 10km east of downtown, according to the Korea Tourism Organization. Bulguksa is further out again, and the KTX station is outside the city altogether.

The [Gyeongju travel guide](/korea/gyeongju) argues for one night rather than a day trip, and the reason is timing. The best night-time sight and the best morning sight are in different places, so where you sleep decides which of the two is a walk and which is a bus.

Buses cost {{gyeongju-city-bus-fare}} a ride, 50 won less with a transit card, and a transfer within 30 minutes is free, according to Gyeongju's transport information centre. Taxis are the catch. Gyeongju Smart Tour notes that a surcharge applies beyond 5km of the Gyeongju Arts Center, which covers the station, Bulguksa and Bomun.

## The old centre: Hwangnidan-gil and Daereungwon

The old centre is Gyeongju at walking pace: burial mounds the size of hills, low-rise lanes and hanok cafés around them. Hwangnidan-gil, in Hwangnam-dong, sits beside the Daereungwon tombs and Cheomseongdae, according to the Korea Tourism Organization.

The low skyline is a rule, not a style. The Korea Times describes how building height limits around the royal tombs kept the neighbourhood low, and how its hanok turned into cafés, craft shops and guesthouses over the past decade. That makes this the area for a hanok stay.

It suits a first visit and a single night best. Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond are open until 10pm, with the last ticket at 9:30pm, and the Korea Tourism Organization singles out the pond after dark. From here that is a walk back, not a bus to catch.

The morning works too. Buses 10 and 11 start from the Hwangnidan-gil public car park and loop out to Bulguksa and Bomun in opposite directions, and bus 711 from the station passes the Hwangnidan-gil stops on its way to Bulguksa. Gyeongju City puts the ride to Bulguksa at 35 to 45 minutes.

It does not suit anyone who wants a large hotel or a quiet street. Hwangnidan-gil has become one of Gyeongju's main attractions, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, and the big resort hotels are out at Bomun.

**Our pick** for one night, and for anyone whose reason for staying is the pond at night.

## Bomun Lake: the resort

Bomun is the resort end of Gyeongju: a lake, big hotels, golf and an amusement park. The Korea Tourism Organization describes it as the city's international tourist district, with luxury hotels, condos and walking paths around Bomunho Lake.

It suits families, travellers with a car and anyone who wants a large hotel with the facilities that come with one. The lakeside is also a cherry blossom sight in April, according to the Korea Tourism Organization.

The trade-off is that nothing historic is walkable. Buses 10 and 11 connect Bomun to the old centre and Bulguksa, and the transport centre lists them up to 50 and 40 minutes apart. Bus 710 links Bomun to the KTX station through the centre, up to an hour apart, from 6:30am to 8:46pm from the station. An evening at Wolji Pond then ends with a wait for a bus or a surcharged taxi.

It does not suit a one-night stay without a car. You spend the night you came for on transport.

**Our pick** for two nights or more with a car, or when the hotel is the holiday.

## Near Bulguksa: the early start

The Bulguksa area is the quiet one: hotels along the road below the temple and little else to do after dark. Its one strength is the morning. Bulguksa opens at 9am all year, and sleeping nearby puts you at the gate before any bus from the centre has arrived.

It suits travellers who care most about Bulguksa and Seokguram, and who want both done before the day gets crowded. Bus 12 runs from Bulguksa up to Seokguram, as set out in [the Gyeongju day trip from Busan](/korea/busan/gyeongju-day-trip-from-busan).

The trade-off is the evening. Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond is back in the centre, a 35- to 45-minute bus ride away, and the taxi back to Bulguksa carries the surcharge.

**Our pick** only for a second night, or when the temples are the whole point.

## Near Gyeongju Station: the one to skip

The area around Gyeongju Station is a rail stop outside the city, not a base. The station was called Singyeongju until 28 December 2023, and older booking pages still use that name for the same place.

The bus into the centre takes 20 to 25 minutes, according to Gyeongju Smart Tour. Buses 50 and 70 are the frequent ones, up to 30 and 25 minutes apart, from 5am until 10:55pm. A taxi downtown costs more than 20,000 won with the surcharge.

It suits one case: a late arrival and an early train out, with no sightseeing in between. For everyone else, staying here means paying the ride in both directions every day, and doing it again for the pond at night.

**Our pick** never for a sightseeing night. If trains are the constraint, stay in the old centre and take bus 50 or 70 to the station.

## How to decide

Answer one question: what do you want to see first, the pond at night or Bulguksa at 9am? For the pond, stay in the old centre, which also handles Bulguksa the next morning by bus. For Bulguksa above everything, stay near the temple. For a resort holiday with a car, stay at Bomun.
