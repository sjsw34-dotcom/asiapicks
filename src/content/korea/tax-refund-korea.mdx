## Who can claim a tax refund in Korea?

Foreign visitors staying in Korea for up to 6 months. The Korea Tourism Organization also includes overseas Koreans who have lived abroad for 2 years or more and are staying 3 months or less.

The refund is for people who do not earn income in Korea. Anyone working in Korea, diplomats, US forces personnel and long-stay residents of 6 months or more are excluded, and so are purchases made under a company name or by an agent.

The tax returned is the VAT, plus the individual consumption tax where an item carries it. Duty-free shopping is a separate system: duty-free shops at the airport and downtown sell without tax in the first place, so there is nothing to claim back.

## What can you buy, and what is the minimum?

Taxable goods from a shop showing a Tax Refund sign, at {{tax-refund-min-purchase}} or more on one receipt. The Korea Tourism Organization lists clothing, shoes, bags, accessories, cosmetics, perfume, home appliances and ginseng as typical refundable goods.

Three groups never qualify. Services that customs cannot see leave the country are out, which covers cooked food and massages. Goods opened or used before you leave are out. Items banned by law, from firearms to cultural properties, are out.

Ask for the refund when you pay, with your passport in hand. Incheon Airport warns that without the refund document from the shop there is no refund later, and that an incomplete form can be refused.

## How does the instant refund at the till work?

The shop checks your passport and charges you the price without the tax. Each purchase must be at least {{tax-refund-min-purchase}} and under {{instant-refund-per-purchase-cap}}, and the instant refunds on one trip must add up to no more than {{instant-refund-total-cap}}. The limits were raised on 1 January 2024, so a guide quoting lower ones is out of date.

The beauty chain most visitors shop at does this at the till: the [Olive Young guide](/korea/seoul/olive-young-for-tourists) covers what its stores ask for and which branches are set up for visitors.

There is nothing to collect later. The goods still have to leave Korea within 3 months of purchase, unopened.

A purchase over the per-purchase cap, or one made after you have used up the trip total, is charged in full. The shop then issues a refund form, and you claim the money in town or at the airport.

## How do you claim a refund form downtown?

At the refund desk or kiosk of the company that issued the form, with your passport, the form and a credit card. The Korea Tourism Organization sets the downtown limit at 6,000,000 won per form, and each downtown desk only pays out its own company's forms.

The money is an advance. Global Tax Free puts a deposit of 110% of the refund on your Visa, Mastercard, Amex or JCB card and cancels it once customs confirms at departure that the goods left Korea. If you skip that step, the deposit stands.

The window for that check is set by the operator, not by law. Global Tax Free asks for customs confirmation within 25 days of the downtown refund, and Global Blue within 21 days, or 15 days for UnionPay. Our pick is to claim downtown only in the last two weeks of a trip, so the airport check falls inside every operator's window.

## How do you get the refund at the airport?

Kiosk first, then customs if the kiosk says so, then the refund counter after passport control. The Korea Customs Service describes the check as export confirmation: you show that the goods are leaving Korea, unopened and unused.

1. Check in for your flight. If the refund goods are going in your checked bag, tell the airline staff, who tag the bag for customs and hand it back to you.
2. Scan your passport and refund forms at a tax refund kiosk in the departure hall, before security.
3. If the kiosk flags your goods for inspection, take your passport, the goods and the forms to the customs desk. At Incheon, the desk is near check-in counters E and J in Terminal 1 and near counters F and G in Terminal 2, both on the 3rd floor.
4. Put the checked bag on the oversized baggage belt next to the customs desk.
5. Clear security and passport control, then collect the money at the refund counter or kiosk airside.

Leave time for it. A customs check with a checked bag means queuing before you can drop that bag, so the refund adds a step to check-in, not to the end of the day.

## Where are the refund counters at Incheon Airport?

Airside, on the 3rd floor of both terminals, after passport control.

| Terminal | Staffed counter | Hours | 24-hour kiosk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terminal 1 | Near Gate 28 | 7am to 10pm | Near Gate 28, and in the Concourse next to the central pharmacy |
| Terminal 2 | Near Gate 253, across from Gate 250 | 7am to 9:30pm | Same location |

The kiosk pays the approved amount when you scan your passport. Incheon Airport says that for a large refund, or if you do not want cash, you can have it paid to a credit card in your own name. The Korea Tourism Organization lists won, US dollars, yuan and yen at the staffed counters, and won, Alipay and WeChat Pay at the kiosks.

Other airports work the same way. The Korea Tourism Organization lists customs desks and refund counters at Gimpo, Gimhae and Jeju. At a port of departure without a counter, you drop the customs-confirmed form in the refund mailbox, with your details and refund method written on it.

## How much money do you get back?

Less than the 10% VAT, because the refund operator takes a fee. Each operator publishes its own table. Global Tax Free's, for example:

| Purchase amount | Refund |
|---|---|
| 15,000 to 29,999 won | 1,000 won |
| 50,000 to 74,999 won | 3,000 won |
| 100,000 to 124,999 won | 7,000 won |
| 200,000 to 224,999 won | 12,000 won |
| 500,000 to 549,999 won | 35,000 won |
| 1,000,000 to 1,099,999 won | 75,000 won |

The share grows with the receipt: 1,000 won back on a 15,000 won purchase is under 7%, and 75,000 won back on a million is 7.5%. For a purchase of 6,000,000 won or more, Global Tax Free refunds 90% of the VAT. Other operators' tables differ by a few hundred won per bracket, so the shop's operator decides the exact figure.

## Can you get the VAT back on hotels or clinic treatments?

On hotels, yes for now. On cosmetic treatments, no longer.

The hotel refund covers room charges for up to 30 nights at a designated hotel, breakfast included only when it is part of the room rate. The Korea Tourism Organization limits it to independent travellers, so package tour groups are excluded, and you must leave Korea within 3 months of the stay. The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism publishes the list of designated hotels every quarter: 110 hotels for July to September 2026. Ask the hotel at check-in rather than assuming it is on the list.

That refund has an end date. Newspim reported that the finance ministry's 2026 tax revision, announced in August, would end it on 30 June 2027 instead of 31 December 2028. As of 15 October 2026 this is a government proposal awaiting the National Assembly, not law.

The refund on cosmetic and dermatology procedures has already gone. The Straits Times reported that it ended on 31 December 2025, after the National Assembly left it out of the tax law revision passed that December. It had returned 10% on treatments from skin rejuvenation to double-eyelid surgery since 2016. A clinic price quoted with the refund taken off is out of date. For beauty bookings that were never part of the scheme, see [head spas in Seoul](/korea/seoul/head-spa-seoul).

## What makes a refund fail?

Opened goods, a missing form, or a missed customs check. The Korea Customs Service only confirms export of goods that are unopened and unused, and Global Blue pays refunds on goods that leave in your own luggage.

Keep the refund goods and their forms together, in hand luggage where you can. Incheon Airport asks you to carry refund goods and their documents on board rather than check them, unless you have the bag tagged at check-in. Global Blue warns that customs rejects a form with missing or wrong details, so check it before you leave the till.
