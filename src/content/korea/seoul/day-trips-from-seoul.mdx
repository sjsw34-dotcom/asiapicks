The ranking weighs what each day gives you against the hours spent getting there. A trip moves up for being unlike anything in Seoul and down for every hour on a bus or train.

## 1. The DMZ: the one trip nothing else replaces

The DMZ is the best day trip from Seoul because it is the only one without a substitute. Fortresses, hanok villages and mountains exist elsewhere in Korea. A viewpoint looking across the border into North Korea does not.

Our pick is a half-day tour from Seoul. Standard tours cover the Third Infiltration Tunnel, Dora Observatory and Imjingak, and the operator handles the transport. The Joint Security Area at Panmunjom, the blue huts, is a different visit: public tours there were suspended in 2023 and a planned 2025 resumption did not happen.

<Figure id="dmz-dora-observatory" />

Going without a tour is possible but awkward. Paju city runs its own DMZ Peace Tourism shuttle from a ticket office inside Imjingak, a 20-minute walk from Imjingang Station on the Gyeongui-Jungang Line. Adult tickets cost 12,200 won with the monorail into the tunnel, or 9,200 won walking down. The drawbacks add up: tickets are sold only from 9am to 2:30pm, the course is closed on Mondays, weekday public holidays and Seollal and Chuseok day, you need an original passport, there is no guide, and the booking website says its English service is not ready yet. Photos and video are banned along the whole route.

It beats Suwon because a historic fortress town has equivalents elsewhere in Korea, while the DMZ's whole point is the border itself. What to check before booking a tour is in the full guide.

## 2. Suwon Hwaseong Fortress: the best trip without a tour

Suwon is the best day trip to do on your own, because it costs almost nothing and needs no booking. Hwaseong Fortress is a UNESCO-listed ring of walls about 5.7km long around Suwon's old centre, and walking the walls is free and open at all hours.

<Figure id="daytrip-suwon-hwaseong-wall" />

Getting there is a subway ride. Suwon Station is on Line 1 and the Suin-Bundang Line, and a ride from Seoul is charged on the ordinary subway fare: 1,550 won for the first 10km on a transit card, plus 100 won for every further 5km. From Suwon Station exit 4, city buses 11 and 13 run to the Hwaseong Haenggung stop, a five-minute walk from the palace. Staying south of the river, the 3000 express bus from Gangnam Station stops at Paldalmun, the fortress's south gate.

Hwaseong Haenggung, the royal palace inside the walls, is the one part with a ticket: 2,000 won for adults, open 9am to 6pm with last entry an hour before closing. The Hwaseong trolley, which runs from Yeonmudae, costs 6,000 won for adults for anyone who would rather ride than walk. A tourist information centre sits near exit 4 of Suwon Station.

It ranks above Nami Island because it works in any season and at any budget, and it does not need the whole day. A morning on the walls leaves the afternoon for Seoul.

## 3. Nami Island: easy, and best in autumn

Nami Island is the easiest trip into the countryside, a tree-lined river island about an hour from Seoul by train. The ITX-Cheongchun train takes about 60 minutes from Yongsan to Gapyeong Station, or about 42 minutes from Cheongnyangni. The ferry wharf is 1.5km from the station, a 20-minute walk or five minutes by bus 10-4 or taxi.

Admission is 19,000 won for adults and includes the return ferry. Paying 29,000 won swaps the ferry out for a motorboat on the way in. The island's own direct shuttle bus from Seoul leaves Hongik University Station exit 4 at 9am, calls at Myeongdong and Dongdaemun History and Culture Park, and reaches the island at 11:30am, returning at 4:30pm.

A tour makes sense if you want to combine the island with other stops, such as the Garden of Morning Calm, in the same day. For the island alone, the train is simpler.

It ranks above Seoraksan because it asks for half the travel time. Seoraksan is the more dramatic landscape, but only for a few weeks a year does the extra distance pay off.

## 4. Seoraksan: the best scenery, if you go in late October

Seoraksan is the most spectacular day trip from Seoul and the most demanding. In autumn its forests turn red and gold, and the 2026 forecast published by the Korea Tourism Organization puts peak colour there on 25 October.

<Figure id="seoraksan-gorge-autumn" />

The distance is the catch. The cable car operator says express buses to Sokcho take about 2 hours 30 minutes and intercity buses about 2 hours 10 minutes, running roughly every 30 minutes. From Sokcho, city buses 7 and 7-1 continue to the park entrance. The Seorak cable car costs 16,000 won return for adults and sells tickets only on site on the day, with no reservations.

Our pick is a tour, because a tour removes the bus connections from a day that is already long. Outside late October, the trip is harder to justify from Seoul. Peak dates for Seoraksan and the rest of the country are in our foliage guide.

It ranks above Jeonju because it offers something no neighbourhood in Seoul can, while Jeonju's hanok streets have a smaller version in Seoul's Bukchon.

## 5. Jeonju Hanok Village: the food-and-hanok day by KTX

Jeonju is the best trip for a day spent walking and eating rather than sightseeing. Jeonju Hanok Village is a large cluster of traditional houses in the centre of the city, free to enter and open all year.

<Figure id="daytrip-jeonju-hanok-roofs" />

The KTX from Yongsan to Jeonju takes about 1 hour 40 minutes, according to the Korea Tourism Organization, and express buses from Seoul's Central City Terminal take about 2 hours 40 minutes. Go independently: the village is best explored on foot, and the train is the fastest link. When tickets go on sale and where to book them is in the KTX booking guide.

It ranks above Gyeongju because it fits into a single day comfortably. Gyeongju is the richer destination, but not as a day trip.

## 6. Gyeongju: worth the trip, but not in a day

Gyeongju ranks last as a day trip because it is the destination that loses most by being rushed. The Silla capital's sights are spread across the city, and the KTX station sits outside the centre, so a day from Seoul covers Bulguksa and one or two central sites between two train rides.

<Figure id="korea-gyeongju-bulguksa" />

Take the KTX from Seoul Station to Gyeongju Station; the station was called Singyeongju until December 2023, and older guides still use that name. Our pick is one night rather than a day trip, which buys the evening at Donggung Palace and Wolji Pond and an early start at Bulguksa. Fares and times are in the KTX booking guide, and the Gyeongju guide covers the station name and what to see.

## Which one should you pick?

Pick the DMZ if you have one free day and have never seen it, and Suwon if you would rather go without a tour or have only half a day. In late October, Seoraksan moves up the list for anyone who wants mountains, and Nami Island is the gentler version of the same autumn trip.
