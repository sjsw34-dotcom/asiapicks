Most arguments about Myeongdong are really arguments about two different questions. One is whether it is a good place to be based for a short first trip to Seoul, and the answer is yes, more clearly than for any other district. The other is whether it is a good place to experience Seoul, and the answer is mostly no. The mistake visitors make is to let the first answer stand in for the second: to book a hotel there for sound reasons and then spend every evening there too.

The numbers explain why both answers hold at once. A Seoul AI Foundation analysis, published by the Seoul Metropolitan Government in July 2026, found that the Myeongdong area drew an average of 100,910 foreign visitors a day in 2025, more than any of the 16 attractions it studied, and that foreign visitors made up 47.1% of everyone in the area. Roughly one person in two on a Myeongdong street is a visitor. That is what makes the district so easy, and it is also what makes it thin.

## Myeongdong is the best logistics in Seoul

A first trip of three or four days is mostly a transport problem, and Myeongdong solves more of it than anywhere else. From Incheon Airport, the Airport Limousine route that serves the district stops at Myeongdong Station, outside hotels on Euljiro and at Namdaemun's edge, for {{airport-limousine-fare-adult}}, so bags go from the carousel to the hotel door without a staircase. By train it is the AREX to Seoul Station, {{arex-allstop-fare-t1}} on the all-stop service from Terminal 1 or {{arex-express-fare}} on the express, then two stops on Line 4, which runs Seoul Station, Hoehyeon, Myeongdong.

<Figure id="seoul-myeongdong-station-gates" />

That same short line is the district's real asset. Seoul Station, for the KTX and the airport train home, is two stops one way. Namdaemun Market is one stop the other way at Hoehyeon. Namsan and its cable car are reached on foot from Myeongdong Station, which is why the [three-day Seoul itinerary](/korea/seoul/seoul-3-day-itinerary) builds its third day around walking from the market up to N Seoul Tower and back down to Myeongdong for dinner. None of this is glamorous, but a base that removes a change of train from every journey saves more time over a week than any view from a hotel window. The full set of airport options, including the late-night buses, is worked through in [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/korea/seoul/incheon-airport-to-seoul).

## It is the most efficient shopping district a visitor can use

Myeongdong is built around the shopping a foreign visitor actually does, and that is a real strength rather than a backhanded compliment. The Korea Tourism Organization describes it as one of Seoul's primary shopping districts, with two main streets running from Myeongdong Station and from Lotte Department Store, lined with brand shops and department stores. Korea.net, reporting on 2025 tourism data, called it the country's top shopping area for foreign tourists and counted 4.5 million international visitors in the first half of the year alone.

<Figure id="olive-young-myeongdong-storefront" />

The density is the point. Olive Young Myeongdong Global, which the company calls the largest of its global specialty stores in Myeongdong, sits by Exit 8 of Myeongdong Station, takes the tax off at the till on purchases of {{tax-refund-min-purchase}} or more with a physical passport, and stays open until 10:30pm every day. Lotte Duty Free's Myeongdong main store is at 30 Eulji-ro, open from 9:30am to 8pm all year according to the Korea Tourism Organization, and Shinsegae Duty Free Myeongdong is at 77 Toegye-ro, a walk from Exit 7 of Hoehyeon Station. A visitor with a list can clear it in one walk, which no other district in Seoul offers. How the refund works in practice, and which Olive Young is worth a detour, is in [Olive Young for tourists](/korea/seoul/olive-young-for-tourists).

## The street food is a snack, not a dinner

The stalls are the image most people carry of Myeongdong, and they are the part most worth approaching with clear expectations. They have been the subject of repeated complaints about overcharging, and the authorities have spent several years responding. In 2024 the Seoul Metropolitan Government, Jung-gu and the police inspected 75 cosmetics shops in the Myeongdong Special Tourist Zone for price labelling, and said restaurants and street vendors would follow. Jung-gu, the district office, has brought in posted prices for the stalls and, in 2024, card terminals, which the Sijung Ilbo reported as a first for street stalls anywhere in the country.

<Figure id="seoul-myeongdong-night-stalls" />

The rules behind the stalls explain why enforcement keeps returning. The Chosun Ilbo reported in June 2025 that Jung-gu has run a real-name permit system since 2016 under which each stall must be one person's only stall, run by that person, on an alternate-day rota, and that many of the roughly 220 stalls on the street were in practice operated by one owner across several pitches. Jung-gu's month-long inspection from 18 June to 17 July 2025 led to 45 administrative penalties, including suspensions and fines, and 136 on-the-spot corrections, according to the Sijung Ilbo, with monthly unannounced checks and mystery shoppers promised after it.

None of that makes the stalls a bad idea. It makes them a snack. A skewer or a pastry in the crowd is part of the evening, and the posted price lets you judge it before you pay. But the visitors themselves point to the weak spot: in the Seoul AI Foundation's analysis of foreign visitors' Google Maps reviews, the improvement areas listed for Myeongdong were pricing, crowd management, bins and restrooms. A district whose main complaint is price is not the place to spend a trip's food budget.

## Myeongdong shows you Seoul's visitors, not Seoul

The deeper limitation is the one in the headline figure. Where foreign visitors are half the crowd, the shops, the menus and the street itself are arranged for visitors, and what you see is a well-run tourist district rather than the city. The same Seoul AI Foundation table makes the contrast plain: foreign visitors made up 9.0% of people in the Hongdae area and 1.9% in Seoul Forest and Seongsu, places where the crowd is mostly Seoul itself.

The contemporary city is close, which is the good news. Euljiro begins at Myeongdong's northern edge, and Korea.net describes its cafes, restaurants and bars in the alleys of old print shops as a departure from Myeongdong's trendiness. The Korea Herald reported in August 2026 that the city now treats Euljiro, still home to small printing and metalworking shops, as a model for night-time tourism, with its spicy sea-snail restaurants and outdoor beer-and-pollack pubs drawing young Koreans as well as visitors. Korea.net also notes that Myeongdong's visitors increasingly carry on to Gwangjang Market for a more Korean meal, and the [Gwangjang Market guide](/korea/seoul/gwangjang-market) covers how to eat there. Further out, [Seongsu-dong](/korea/seoul/seongsu-dong) is the clearest view of what young Seoul is building now.

## Use it for what it is good at

The case for Myeongdong is strongest when it is narrow. Sleep there if the trip is short, if you land with luggage, if a KTX leaves Seoul Station in the morning, or if shopping is a large part of the plan. Do the shopping there, early in the day before the streets fill. Then leave in the evening, because the things Myeongdong is weak at, dinner and a sense of the city, are a short walk or ride away.

**Our pick** is to treat Myeongdong as a base and a shopping stop, and to eat most of your dinners in Euljiro, Jongno or Hongdae. If you are still choosing between Myeongdong and the main alternative, [Hongdae vs Myeongdong](/korea/seoul/hongdae-vs-myeongdong) compares them station by station, and [where to stay in Seoul](/korea/seoul/where-to-stay-in-seoul) sets Myeongdong against Jongno and Gangnam as well.

If you would rather hand the evening to someone who knows the old city, a guided food walk through Jongno does exactly that.
