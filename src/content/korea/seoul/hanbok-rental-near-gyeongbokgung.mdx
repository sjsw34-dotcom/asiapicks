## Do you get into the palaces free in hanbok?

Yes. Korea's palace authority lists people wearing hanbok among those admitted free, and its English admission page names the four palaces, Gyeongbokgung, Changdeokgung, Changgyeonggung and Deoksugung, plus Jongmyo Shrine. The rule is the same for foreign visitors: the stated aim is to spread hanbok worldwide.

What counts is the form. The official guideline accepts both traditional and modern everyday hanbok, provided you wear a top and a bottom, the jacket keeps its wrap collar, and trousers follow the traditional cut. Any skirt style is fine. A jeogori with jeans, hanbok trousers with a T-shirt, or an outer robe worn on its own does not qualify, and hanbok that shows too much is refused.

Two points trip people up. The fusion hanbok that rental shops around the palaces offer is judged by those same rules rather than banned outright, although heavily altered one-piece dresses do not pass, as Yonhap reported in May 2026. And gender does not matter: the rule tying hanbok to the wearer's gender was removed in July 2019.

## Where do you rent hanbok near Gyeongbokgung?

On Sajik-ro, next to Gyeongbokgung Station. Several shops listed on Creatrip sit within about a minute's walk of the station, so you can change and walk to the palace without a taxi or a second subway ride.

What a rental includes varies more than the price of a ticket would suggest, so check three things before booking:

- **Hair styling.** YES Hanbok and Hanboknam include it; at other shops it is an extra.
- **ID or deposit.** Daehan Hanbok asks for neither, while Hanboknam asks you to leave a passport or other ID.
- **Booking notice.** The shops above take bookings at least a day ahead, and Hanboknam two days.

## How should you time the palace visit?

Around the guard ceremony, and not on a Tuesday. Gyeongbokgung is closed on Tuesdays, and open from 9am to 6pm in September and October, with last entry at 5pm, and from 9am to 5pm in November and December. The changing of the royal guard runs at 10:00 and 14:00 and takes about 20 minutes, so a 4-hour rental starting around 9:30 covers the morning ceremony and the palace.

The ceremony is not guaranteed. It was suspended under a severe heat warning in August 2026, so check the palace notices on a very hot day. One autumn 2026 notice affects photos: the terrace in front of Geunjeongjeon, the throne hall, is closed to visitors from 2 September to 31 October to protect its stone railings.

Gyeongbokgung also opens at night from 2 September to 2 October 2026, except on Mondays and Tuesdays, from 7pm to 9:30pm with last entry at 8:30pm. Tickets are 3,000 won and are booked online in advance, but 300 on-site tickets a day are kept for foreign visitors with a passport, and visitors in hanbok are admitted free after a check against the guideline.

## Where can you walk in hanbok afterwards?

Through Bukchon, but by 5pm. Jongno-gu bans tourists from the red zone around Bukchon-ro 11-gil from 5pm to 10am, and has enforced the rule since 1 March 2025 with a {{bukchon-red-zone-fine}} fine. Taking photos or video counts as tourism even for people otherwise exempt, and the rule applies to foreign visitors, who can pay on the spot or before leaving the country.

The yellow zone around Bukchon-ro 12-gil asks visitors to keep to the same hours without fining them. An all-day rental is better finished in Insadong, or at Gyeongbokgung's night opening while it runs, than in Bukchon's lanes.
