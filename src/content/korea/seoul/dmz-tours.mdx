## Can you go on your own?

No. Independent entry into the DMZ is not permitted, so every visit runs through an authorised operator. That is why the DMZ is one of the few things near Seoul you cannot simply take a train to and look at, and why the entire market for it is organised tours.

Practically, that means the decision is not whether to book a tour but which one, and the difference between them is bigger than the listings suggest.

## The DMZ and the JSA are not the same visit

This is the distinction that decides whether you get what you expected.

The **JSA** at Panmunjom is the Joint Security Area: the blue huts, the soldiers facing each other across the line, the images that made the DMZ famous. Public tours there have been suspended since December 2023. A resumption was planned for March 2025 and did not happen, and in October 2025 even the restricted tours for military officials, distinguished visitors and media were suspended.

The **DMZ tour** that operators run from Seoul goes somewhere else entirely: the Third Infiltration Tunnel, the Dora Observatory with its view north, and Imjingak near the Freedom Bridge. These run normally.

Both are worth seeing. They are not interchangeable, and only one of them is currently available.

## What to check before you book

Listings that advertise a JSA or Panmunjom tour are worth reading carefully, because the JSA has not been open to the public for over two years. What some of them sell is a standard DMZ tour with a JSA attempt that depends on conditions, which in practice means the DMZ tour you would have bought anyway.

Two things settle it. Does the itinerary name the Third Tunnel, Dora Observatory and Imjingak, and nothing north of them? Then it is a DMZ tour, correctly priced as one. Does it promise the blue huts? Then check what it commits to and what it refunds if access is refused.

Bring your passport. Identity is checked before entry to the controlled area, and operators ask for the passport rather than any other document.

## Is it worth the day?

Yes, with one expectation adjusted. A half-day tour is largely a bus journey with three stops, and the Third Tunnel is a steep 500m walk down and back in a helmet. What makes it land is not spectacle but proximity: the observatory looks at ordinary hills that happen to be in another country.

Our pick is a half-day tour rather than a full day. The full-day versions add a suspension bridge or a shopping stop, and the additional hours rarely add to what the morning already showed you.
