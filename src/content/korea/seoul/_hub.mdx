## Where to start if this is your first trip

Two decisions shape a Seoul trip more than any other: which neighbourhood you sleep in, and whether you are comfortable on the subway. Seoul is large and its attractions are scattered, so a well-placed base saves more time than any itinerary trick.

The city sits on both banks of the Han River. North of the river holds the five Joseon palaces, the old hanok quarters and the student and nightlife districts around Hongdae. South of the river is Gangnam, which is newer, wider and built around business and shopping rather than historic sights. Most first-time visitors spend the majority of their time north of the river.

## Do you need a visa or a K-ETA?

Nationals of visa-free countries and regions can enter South Korea without a K-ETA until 31 December 2026. The Ministry of Justice extended this temporary exemption to support the tourism industry, and the Korea Tourism Organization published the extension in December 2025. Travellers from countries that are not on the visa-free list still need a visa, and the exemption does not change that.

Applying for a K-ETA is still possible during the exemption and carries one practical benefit: K-ETA holders are not asked to fill in the paper arrival card. Check the official K-ETA site for the current country list before booking, because the exemption has an explicit end date and has been extended year by year rather than made permanent.

## How much does getting around cost?

A Seoul subway ride starts at 1,550 won with a transit card, or 1,650 won paying cash for a single-journey ticket. The base fare rose from 1,400 won on 28 June 2025 and applies across Seoul, Incheon and Gyeonggi Province, so the same fare structure covers most of the capital region. Longer journeys add to the base fare by distance.

One caution on that number. Seoul's official English tourism site still lists the pre-increase fare of 1,400 won, and several widely-read guides repeat it. A page quoting 1,400 won is out of date rather than describing a cheaper ticket, so budget on 1,550 won. We took the current figure from the Korea Herald's report on the day the increase took effect, listed in the sources below.

A transit card is worth buying on arrival rather than paying cash each trip. The card is cheaper per ride, and transfers between subway lines and buses are handled automatically when you tap out and tap in again. Our pick for the first thing to sort out after landing is the airport transfer, covered in [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/korea/seoul/incheon-airport-to-seoul), followed by the rest of our [Seoul transportation guides](/korea/seoul/transportation).

## When to go

Autumn is the most reliable stretch of weather, and the numbers are lopsided enough to plan around. October in Seoul averages a low of 9.9°C and a high of 20.1°C with roughly 50mm of rain across the whole month. July averages 415mm, the wettest month of the year by a wide margin. Those are 1991 to 2020 climate normals, so they describe the pattern rather than any single year.

Spring is the other mild window: April averages 7.4°C to 17.8°C with about 75mm of rain, and brings cherry blossom and crowds to match. Summer is hot and humid, with the changma rains concentrated from late June through July. Winter is cold and dry, averaging 15mm of precipitation in January, and it buys low-season room rates and the shortest queues at the palaces.

Our pick is late September through early November. No season is unworkable, because the subway and the covered markets make bad weather manageable, but October is the month where the weather stops being a variable.
