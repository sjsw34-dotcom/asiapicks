N Seoul Tower stands on the summit of Namsan, the hill between Myeongdong and Itaewon. Private cars have been barred from the summit road since 2005, so every visitor arrives by cable car, by one of two circulating buses or on foot. The choice comes down to the queue, the fare and how many stairs you want.

## How much is the N Seoul Tower observatory, and when is it open?

The observatory costs {{n-seoul-tower-observatory-adult}} for adults, and the tower counts everyone aged 13 and over as an adult. Children aged 3 to 12 and seniors aged 65 and over pay 23,000 won, and children under 36 months go in free, one per accompanying adult.

It opens every day of the year at 10am. Closing time is 10:30pm on weekdays and 11pm at weekends and on public holidays, and new visitors are turned away for the last 30 minutes. The tower warns that hours can change with the weather.

The ticket covers the observatory only. The love locks around the base of the tower need no ticket, and the walk up costs nothing.

## How do you take the Namsan cable car?

Leave Myeongdong Station on Line 4 by exit 3 and walk 10 to 15 minutes uphill, along the street to the right of the Pacific Hotel, to the lower station. The cable car runs from 10am to 11pm every day and climbs to the summit in about three minutes.

A return ticket is {{namsan-cable-car-adult-return}} for adults and a one-way ticket {{namsan-cable-car-adult-one-way}}. Children from 36 months to 12 years, seniors and people with disabilities pay 11,500 won return or 9,000 won one way. The operator sells tickets only at the station, and its home page showed the ticket office closing at 10:30pm.

Two things shorten the walk to the lower station. Namsan Ormi, a free inclined lift by the Namsan Tunnel 3 monument, reached from exit 4 of Myeongdong Station with a left turn at the Hoehyeon crossroads, runs from 9am to 11pm and reaches the cable car building in about two minutes; it stops on Monday mornings from 9am to 2pm for maintenance, and in strong wind or heavy rain. The cable car company also runs a free shuttle bus from exit 1 of Myeongdong Station, about every 10 minutes from noon until the last departure at 8:50pm, with longer gaps in some afternoon and evening hours.

The queue is the drawback. The Seoul city government says waits of one to two hours are common at weekends and in peak seasons, and the operator itself advises that mornings are quieter than afternoons.

## How do you take the bus to N Seoul Tower?

Take the Namsan circulating bus 01A or 01B from exit 2 of Chungmuro Station, on Lines 3 and 4. Both routes run from 6:30am to 11pm and stop at the tower; 01A comes every 9 minutes and 01B every 14 minutes, according to N Seoul Tower.

The buses take transit cards only, not cash. N Seoul Tower's site lists the fare as 1,500 won, the standard Seoul city bus fare on a card, while Seoul's fare table charges 1,400 won on circulating routes, so you may be charged the lower figure. A card bought for the subway works, and the rules for transfers are in [Seoul subway fares and transfers](/korea/seoul/subway-fares-and-transfers).

The two routes differ after the summit. 01B is a short loop that also stops by the cable car station and returns to the Namsan Yejang bus transfer car park near Myeongdong. 01A carries on through Namdaemun Market, Gwanghwamun, Gyeongbokgung, Anguk and Insadong before looping back, which makes it a cheap way down to the palace district.

The bus does not reach the very top. Members of a Seoul city advisory committee who inspected the summit in August 2026 walked the roughly 350m of steep uphill from the summit bus stop to the top, and the city cites that stretch when it argues for the gondola.

## Can you walk up to N Seoul Tower?

Yes, and since July 2025 it is much shorter from Myeongdong. Seoul opened the North Forest Path, a 0.5km staircase of wooden decking from the North Circular Road to the summit, and the city says it cuts the walk from the Myeongdong area from about an hour to 20 minutes.

The path is all steps: a citizen reporter for the city counted 954 of them. That reporter walked down from the summit to exit 3 of Myeongdong Station in about 25 minutes and estimated 25 to 35 minutes for the climb, depending on fitness. Three rest decks on the way have views over the city.

The older routes are gentler and longer. The same reporter puts the walk from the North Circular Road entrance near the National Theater of Korea at 35 to 40 minutes, and about an hour from Chungmuro or Dongguk University Station. For autumn colour on the way, [Korea's autumn foliage guide](/korea/autumn-foliage) covers the Namsan Southern Walking Path.

## Which way up should you choose?

Our pick: walk or take the bus up, and use the cable car only if it is a weekday morning or you need the ride itself. The bus costs a fraction of the cable car and runs until 11pm, and the North Forest Path is quicker than most cable car queues for anyone comfortable with stairs.

The cable car is the right choice for anyone who would rather not climb, and for anyone who wants the view on the ride. On a weekend, go before noon or expect to queue.

## When is the best time to go up N Seoul Tower?

Late afternoon on a weekday, staying until after dark. The cable car operator recommends the hours from sunset into the night for the summit, and the observatory stays open until 10:30pm on weekdays, so there is time to see the city both in daylight and lit up.

Avoid weekend afternoons if you plan to ride the cable car, because the operator says waits grow at weekends and afternoons are busier than mornings.

## Where are the love locks?

On the fences and railings around the base of the tower. The Korea Tourism Organization traces the tradition to two padlocks attached to the tower fence when it reopened in 2006; today, it says, the fences are nearly buried in locks, and K-pop fans now hang their own decorated with photocards and messages.

Seeing the locks costs nothing and needs no observatory ticket.

## What happened to the Namsan gondola?

It is on hold, and may not be built. Seoul announced the gondola in June 2023 to link Yejang Park, near Myeongdong Station, with the summit: 832m in about three minutes, in 25 cabins of 10 people each, carrying more than 2,000 people an hour.

Construction began in 2024 and stopped in October 2024, when a court granted the cable car operator's request to suspend the city's planning decision. The operator won the case at first instance in December 2025 and again on appeal on 17 September 2026. On 12 October 2026 the city said it would take the case to the Supreme Court, while also asking the national government to relax the height limit for structures in urban nature parks.

For a trip in 2026 or 2027, plan on the cable car, the buses or the stairs.
