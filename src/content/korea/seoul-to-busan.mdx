**Our pick is the KTX from Seoul Station.** The one condition that changes it: if you are staying in Gangnam or anywhere south of the Han River, take the same kind of train from Suseo instead. It is faster and slightly cheaper.

Times are scheduled, station to station: add the ride to the station or airport at each end.

## Why is the KTX the best choice for most travellers?

Because it is the fastest option from city centre to city centre, and it runs on average about every 20 minutes from early morning to late evening. The first train leaves Seoul Station at 5:13am and the last daily one at 10:28pm, and both ends are stations on the subway: Seoul Station on Lines 1 and 4, Busan Station on Busan's Line 1.

The fare is {{ktx-seoul-busan-standard}} in standard class and 78,900 won in first class. Korail cut it from {{ktx-seoul-busan-standard.previous}} on 1 September 2026, when it took over the SRT and set every high-speed fare to the cheaper SRT scale.

Check the journey time before picking a departure. Of the 74 departures from Seoul Station on the September timetable, counting trains that run only on some days of the week, 11 take 2 hours 30 minutes or less, most take 2 hours 31 to 3 hours, and 17 take longer than 3 hours because they make more stops. The fastest, 2 hours 18 minutes, leave at 8:58am daily and at 3:45pm.

**Our pick** for anyone staying north of the river, in Myeongdong, Jongno, Hongdae or around Seoul Station.

## When is Suseo, the old SRT, the better station?

When your hotel is in Gangnam, Jamsil or anywhere in southeast Seoul. Suseo is on subway Line 3 and the Suin-Bundang Line, so getting there from the south of the city is usually quicker than crossing to Seoul Station.

Suseo trains are also marginally cheaper and faster. Standard class to Busan costs {{ktx-suseo-busan-standard}} and first class 76,700 won, and the fastest train, the 9am, takes 2 hours 11 minutes. Thirteen of the 44 Suseo departures on the timetable take 2 hours 30 minutes or less, and every one ends at Busan Station, the same as trains from Seoul Station.

The catch is frequency: about 34 trains on Monday to Thursday, against about 55 from Seoul Station. Since the merger the SRT name has gone, the trains are called KTX-Sancheon, and they appear in the same Korail search as trains from Seoul Station. Advice to book the SRT on a separate website dates from before September 2026.

**Our pick** for anyone staying south of the river.

## How far ahead should you book the train?

Book as soon as your dates are fixed, and on the morning sales open for a Friday, Sunday or holiday. Since 1 October 2026 Korail sells tickets from 7am two months before the departure date, double the old one-month window. The change was made partly for foreign visitors, who were booking flights two months out but could not yet book the train.

Korail's English website and the KORAIL+ app sell both Seoul Station and Suseo trains. Trip.com sells KTX tickets in English and suits anyone who would rather not open a Korail account; compare its checkout total with Korail's fare before paying.

## When does the express bus make sense?

When the fare matters more than the time, or when the train is sold out. An excellent-class express bus, with 28 wider seats instead of the usual 45, costs {{express-bus-seoul-busan-excellent}}, about 11,000 won less than the KTX. Premium buses cost 56,200 won, and the three regular buses of the day, in the late afternoon and evening, 29,100 won.

The scheduled journey from Seoul Express Bus Terminal to Busan is 4 hours over 380.4km, almost twice the fastest train, and traffic can add more. Buses leave around the clock: 32 a day, including late-night departures between 10pm and 4am, which are charged at higher late-night fares of 51,900 won for excellent class and 67,400 won for premium.

The Seoul terminal is at Express Bus Terminal subway station, on Lines 3, 7 and 9. The Busan terminal is at Nopo, the northern end of Busan's Line 1, so a hotel near Haeundae or Busan Station is a long subway ride further. Tickets are sold on the KOBUS website, which has an English version, and at the terminal.

**Our pick** for a tight budget, or for a late-night departure after the last train.

## Is flying from Seoul to Busan worth it?

Rarely, for a trip that starts in central Seoul. Flights from Gimpo to Gimhae are scheduled at 1 hour 5 to 1 hour 20 minutes, but both airports are outside the city centres, and the check-in and security time at Gimpo comes on top. Door to door, the train is usually as quick and less hassle.

Korean Air, Air Busan, Trinity Airways and Jeju Air flew the route, with about 20 departures from Gimpo between 7am and 8pm on the schedule running to 24 October 2026. The winter schedule starts on 25 October, so check the airline for later dates. Fares vary by airline and day, and we did not find a published standard fare to quote.

A flight makes sense when you are already at or near Gimpo, or when Busan is only the airport you leave from. Long-haul travellers arriving at Incheon should read [Incheon Airport to Seoul](/korea/seoul/incheon-airport-to-seoul) first: most trips begin in Seoul, and the KTX south comes later.

## Which option suits which traveller?

- **First trip, staying central:** KTX from Seoul Station.
- **Staying in Gangnam or Jamsil:** KTX from Suseo.
- **Lowest fare, no rush:** the express bus, or a regular bus at 29,100 won if the times suit.
- **After the last train:** a late-night bus from Seoul Express Bus Terminal.
- **Already at Gimpo Airport:** a flight to Gimhae.
